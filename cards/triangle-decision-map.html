Your route

    Q1 says no? Look again

    Hidden triangles

    When the question shows a shape, a scene or a graph, the right triangle is already there. The gold triangle is the one to draw.

    Rectangle or square

    The diagonal cuts it into two right triangles. d² = length² + width².

    Isosceles triangle

    Drop the height from the top. It cuts the base in half and the top angle in half.

    Rhombus or kite

    The diagonals cross at 90°. A rhombus gives four equal right triangles.

    Trapezium

    Drop the two heights. You get a rectangle in the middle and a right triangle at each end.

    Tangent to a circle

    The radius meets the tangent at 90°. Join the centre to the touching point.

    Semicircle

    An angle drawn from the two ends of a diameter to the curve is always 90°.

    Chord

    The line from the centre that meets a chord at 90° cuts the chord in half.

    Two points on a graph

    Across is the change in x, up is the change in y. Distance = √(Δx² + Δy²).

    Elevation and depression

    Both are measured from the horizontal. Depression from the top d = elevation from the bottom e (alternate angles).

    Bearings

    North and East meet at 90°. Split the journey into a North part and an East part.

    Ladder against a wall

    The wall is vertical and the ground is horizontal, so they meet at 90°. The ladder is the hypotenuse.

    Regular polygon

    Join the centre to two corners: angle = 360° ÷ n. Halve that isosceles triangle. Interior angle = (n − 2) × 180° ÷ n.

    The map on real exam questions

    Worked past papers

    Six Edexcel 4MA1 questions from T48 and T49, each shown as its route through the map. Every number was checked by calculation.

    JAN 2021 · 2HR · Q133 marks · T48 V3

    Two balloons are 500 m apart. The angle of depression of B from A is 23°. Find the vertical height of A above B.

    Q1 No → horizontal at A→Q3 Yes→Q4 Angle given → SOH CAH TOA
    1. Draw the horizontal through A. The 23° sits between it and AB.
    2. From the 23°: H = 500 and you want O, so use sin.
    3. h = 500 sin 23° = 195.36…
    Answer (1 d.p.)195.4 m
    JUN 2022 · 2HR · Q105 marks · T48 V4

    Right triangle ABP inside a regular hexagon: AB = 5 cm, BP = 2 cm, angle ABP = 90°. Find angle PAF.

    Q1 No → hexagon angle→Q3 Yes→Q4 Angle wanted → tan
    1. Angle PAF is not inside one triangle. Split it: PAF = BAF − BAP.
    2. Interior angle of a regular hexagon: (6 − 2) × 180° ÷ 6 = 120°
    3. From angle BAP: O = 2, A = 5, so tan BAP = 2 ÷ 5 → 21.80°
    4. PAF = 120° − 21.80° = 98.19…°
    Answer (3 s.f.)98.2°
    NOV 2025 · 1H · Q114 marks · T48 V4

    Right angle at B, AC = 21 cm, BC = 15 cm, D on AB with DB = 9 cm. Find angle ACD.

    Q2 No → chain 2 triangles→Q3 Yes ×2→SOH CAH TOA ×2→subtract
    1. Triangle ABC, from C: A = 15, H = 21, so cos ACB = 15 ÷ 21 → 44.42°
    2. Triangle DBC, from C: O = 9, A = 15, so tan DCB = 9 ÷ 15 → 30.96°
    3. ACD = 44.42° − 30.96° = 13.45…°

    Shortcut: you never need AB. Using the given 15 and 21 avoids a rounded √216.

    Answer (1 d.p.)13.5°
    JAN 2022 · 2H · Q236 marks · T49 V4

    PQ = 1.6 cm, PR = 4.2 cm, angle PRQ = 18°, angle PQR is obtuse. Find the area of triangle PQR.

    Q3 No→Q4 Pair (PQ, 18°) → sine rule→180°→½ab sin C
    1. PQ = 1.6 is opposite the 18°: that is the full pair.
    2. sin Q ÷ 4.2 = sin 18° ÷ 1.6 → sin Q = 0.8112
    3. sin⁻¹ gives 54.2°, but Q is obtuse: Q = 180° − 54.2° = 125.8°
    4. P = 180° − 18° − 125.8° = 36.2°, the angle between PR and PQ
    5. Area = ½ × 4.2 × 1.6 × sin 36.2° = 1.98…
    Answer (3 s.f.)1.98 cm²
    MAY/JUN 2023 · 1HR · Q256 marks · T49 V1

    Sides (2x − 1) and (2x + 1) with 30° between them. The area is x² + x − 3.75. Find the largest angle.

    Area given → equation→SAS → cosine rule→SSS → cosine rule
    1. ½(2x − 1)(2x + 1) sin 30° = x² + x − 3.75
    2. x² − 0.25 = x² + x − 3.75 → x = 3.5, so the sides are 6 and 8
    3. BC² = 6² + 8² − 2(6)(8) cos 30° → BC = 4.106
    4. Largest angle is opposite the longest side (8), at B: cos B = (6² + 4.106² − 8²) ÷ (2 × 6 × 4.106)
    Answer (nearest degree)103°
    JAN 2020 · 1HR · Q216 marks · T49 V4

    Prism ABCDEF: angle BEC = 40°, CE = 13 cm, AC = 6 cm, area of ABC = 22 cm², angle ACB obtuse. Find AB.

    Q2 No → chain→hidden 90° at B→sin→area backwards→cosine rule
    1. Edge BE is perpendicular to the end face, so angle CBE = 90°.
    2. From the 40°: H = 13, want O: BC = 13 sin 40° = 8.356
    3. ½ × 6 × 8.356 × sin C = 22 → sin C = 0.8776
    4. sin⁻¹ gives 61.4°, but C is obtuse: C = 118.6°
    5. AB² = 6² + 8.356² − 2(6)(8.356) cos 118.6°
    Answer (1 d.p.)12.4 cm
    Where marks are lost

    Exam traps

    Each one has cost real marks on these two topics. Read the fix, not just the trap.

    sin⁻¹ when the angle is obtuse

    Your calculator only returns angles up to 90°.

    “Obtuse” in the question, or a wide angle in the diagram, means 180° − your answer.

    Jan 2022 2H Q23 · Jan 2020 1HR Q21

    Pythagoras on a triangle with no right angle

    a² + b² = c² only works with a 90°.

    Answer Q3 first. No right angle means sine or cosine rule.

    Adding when you want a short side

    The hypotenuse came out shorter than your answer.

    Short side = subtract. Hypotenuse = add. The hypotenuse is always the biggest number.

    Rounding in the middle

    Two-triangle questions drift off the mark scheme.

    Keep the full value on the calculator (ANS or memory). Round once, at the end.

    T48 V4 · T49 V4

    Calculator in RAD or GRAD

    Every trig answer is wrong and you can’t see why.

    Check sin 30 = 0.5 before the first trig question.

    Wrong angle in ½ab sin C

    Using an angle that is not between the two sides.

    C must sit between a and b. If it doesn’t, find the right angle first with the 180° rule or the sine rule.

    Cosine rule in the wrong order

    Working out (b² + c² − 2bc) first, then × cos A.

    Type it in one go: b² + c² − 2bc cos A, then press √ for a side.

    Sine rule upside down

    Putting x/sin 31 when x is an angle.

    Unknown angle → angles on top: sin x ÷ 31 = sin 72° ÷ 54.

    T49 practice P12

    Depression angle in the wrong place

    Putting the angle at the bottom against the vertical.

    Draw the horizontal at the top. Depression from the top equals elevation from the bottom.

    Jan 2021 2HR Q13

    Using your own answer when a given value works

    A rounded √216 makes the final angle drift.

    Pick the ratio that uses the numbers printed in the question.

    Nov 2025 1H Q11

    Largest angle at the wrong corner

    Finding an angle that is not the biggest.

    The largest angle is opposite the longest side. Find that side first.

    May/Jun 2023 1HR Q25

    Stopping at the number

    The question asked for a cost, a time, “is it safe?” or “who arrives first?”.

    Finish with the comparison or the money, plus a short sentence.
