  1. Part (a) | Step 1

    The first class has width 15 g and frequency 9, so its density is \(9/15=0.6\). Its height is 6 small grid squares, so each vertical square represents density \(0.1\).

    Frequency is class width multiplied by frequency density.

  2. Part (a) | Step 2

    The classes below 175 g have densities \(0.6,2.9,4.2,3.4\) and widths \(15,10,5,5\), respectively.

    Read the bar heights using the calibrated vertical scale.

  3. Part (a) | Step 3
    \[N=15(0.6)+10(2.9)+5(4.2)+5(3.4)=9+29+21+17=76.\]

    Add the frequencies, not the heights.

  4. Part (b) | Step 1

    Above 180 g, the frequency from 180 to 185 is \(5(2.4)=12\), from 185 to 190 is \(5(5)=25\), and from 190 to 200 is \(10(1.5)=15\).

    Use only half of the 175-185 bar and all of the two heavier classes.

  5. Part (b) | Step 2
    \[P(W>190\mid W>180)=\frac{15}{12+25+15}=\frac{15}{52}.\]

    Restrict the denominator to the large apples.

Final answer

(a) \(76\text{ apples}\)

(b) \(15/52\)
