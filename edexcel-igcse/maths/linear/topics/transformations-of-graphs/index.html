  1. Part (a) | Step 1

    The graph has maximum 3 and minimum -3, so \(|a|=3\). Since \(a<0\), \(a=-3\).

    The amplitude is half the vertical range.

  2. Part (a) | Step 2

    At \(x=0\), \(y=-3\), so \(-3\sin b^\circ=-3\) and \(\sin b^\circ=1\). Hence \(b=90\) for \(0<b<360\).

    The minimum at x=0 fixes the phase when the amplitude is negative.

  3. Part (b) | Step 1

    To use the original input \(p\), set \(2x=p\), giving \(x=p/2\).

    The replacement of x by 2x halves horizontal coordinates.

  4. Part (b) | Step 2

    The corresponding output is \(f(p)+5=q+5\).

    Adding 5 translates every output upwards by 5.

Final answer

(a) \(a=-3,\qquad b=90\).

(b) \(\left(\frac p2,q+5\right)\).

19. Jun 2026 P2H · Q26

Hard5 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Part (a) | Step 1

    For \(y=3f(x)\), x-coordinates are unchanged and y-coordinates are multiplied by \(3\). Thus \((-5,4)\mapsto(-5,12)\).

    The vertical scale factor is positive, so a minimum remains a minimum.

  2. Part (b) | Step 1

    For \(Q(x)=2-(x+3)^2\), \[Q(-x)=2-(-x+3)^2=2-(3-x)^2=R(x).\] Replacing \(x\) by \(-x\) reflects the graph in the y-axis.

    The point mapping is (x,y) to (-x,y).

  3. Part (c) | Step 1

    The maximum is \(5\) and the minimum is \(1\), so \[a=\frac{5-1}{2}=2,\qquad c=\frac{5+1}{2}=3.\]

    The positive amplitude is half the range; the vertical shift is the midline.

  4. Part (c) | Step 2

    A maximum occurs at \(x=-60^\circ\). For positive \(a\), cosine is maximal when \(x+b=0^\circ\) modulo \(360^\circ\), giving \(b=60\) in the stated interval. Hence \(y=2\cos(x+60)^\circ+3\).

    Read the phase from a maximum and apply 0<b<360.

Final answer

(a) \((-5,12)\)

(b) Reflection in the y-axis.

(c) \(a=2,\quad b=60,\quad c=3\).
