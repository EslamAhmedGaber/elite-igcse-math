1. Jun 2026 P1H · Q17

Medium3 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Solution | Step 1
    \[\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5,\qquad(4-\sqrt{20})(5-2\sqrt5)=(4-2\sqrt5)(5-2\sqrt5).\]

    Simplify the surd first.

  2. Solution | Step 2
    \[20-8\sqrt5-10\sqrt5+20=40-18\sqrt5.\]

    Expand; the product of the two surd terms is positive 20.

  3. Solution | Step 3
    \[18\sqrt5=\sqrt{18^2\times5}=\sqrt{1620},\quad(4-\sqrt{20})(5-2\sqrt5)=40-\sqrt{1620}.\]

    Thus the required integer values are a=40 and b=1620.

Final answer

\(40-\sqrt{1620}\), so \(a=40,\ b=1620\).

3. Jun 2022 P1H · Q16

Medium3 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Proof

    Rationalise the denominator: \[ \frac{12}{\sqrt2-1} =\frac{12(\sqrt2+1)}{(\sqrt2-1)(\sqrt2+1)} \]

    \[ =12(\sqrt2+1)=12\sqrt2+12 \]

    Also, \[ (\sqrt2)^5=(\sqrt2)^4\sqrt2=4\sqrt2 \]

    So the left side is \[ 12\sqrt2+12-4\sqrt2=8\sqrt2+12 \]

    And \[ 2\sqrt{32}+12=2(4\sqrt2)+12=8\sqrt2+12 \]

    So both sides are equal.

Final answer

Shown.

11. Jun 2022 P1HR · Q21

Medium4 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Simplify surd
    \[ \frac{3+\sqrt8}{(\sqrt2-1)^2} \]
  2. Simplify surd

    Since \(\sqrt8=2\sqrt2\),

    \[ \frac{3+2\sqrt2}{(\sqrt2-1)^2} \]
  3. Simplify surd

    Also

    \[ (\sqrt2-1)^2=3-2\sqrt2 \]
  4. Simplify surd

    So

    \[ \frac{3+2\sqrt2}{3-2\sqrt2}\times \frac{3+2\sqrt2}{3+2\sqrt2} =\frac{(3+2\sqrt2)^2}{9-8} \]
    \[ =(3+2\sqrt2)^2=9+12\sqrt2+8 \]
    \[ =17+12\sqrt2=17+\sqrt{288} \]
Final answer

\(17+\sqrt{288}\)
