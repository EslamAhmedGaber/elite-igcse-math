1. WMA12/01 October 2024 · Q7

Easy6 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Identify Jem's sequence

    Jem's payments are arithmetic with first term $20$ and common difference $0.50$.

  2. Show month 100

    The month $100$ payment is $20+99(0.50)=69.50$, so Jem pays £69.50.

  3. Sum Jem's payments

    $S_{300}=\dfrac{300}{2}\{2(20)+299(0.50)\}=150(189.5)=28425$.

  4. Model Kim's payments

    Let the common ratio be $r$. Since the first payment is $20$ and the month $300$ payment is $250$, $20r^{299}=250$.

  5. Find the ratio

    Thus $r^{299}=12.5$ and $r=12.5^{1/299}$.

  6. Sum Kim's payments

    $S_B=20\dfrac{r^{300}-1}{r-1}$ with $r=12.5^{1/299}$, giving $S_B\approx27362.95$.

  7. Find the difference

    The difference is $28425-27362.95\approx1062.05$, which is £1,060 to the nearest £10.

Final answer

$(a)$ Proven. $\quad (b)$ £28,425. $\quad (c)$ £1,060.

  1. Substitute t equals two

    $D=8+5\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi(2)}{6}+3\right)=8+5\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}+3\right)$.

  2. Evaluate

    $D=4.064\ldots$, so the depth at 2 am is just over $4$ metres.

  3. Set depth equal to six

    $6=8+5\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{6}+3\right)$, so $\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{6}+3\right)=-\dfrac25$.

  4. Use the time interval

    After midday means $t>12$. Then $\dfrac{\pi t}{6}+3$ is greater than $2\pi+3$.

  5. Choose the first suitable angle

    The first angle after $2\pi+3$ with sine $-\dfrac25$ is $3\pi+\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac25\right)$.

  6. Solve for t

    $\dfrac{\pi t}{6}+3=3\pi+\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac25\right)$, so $t=13.056\ldots$ hours after midnight.

  7. Convert to clock time

    $0.056\ldots$ hours is about $3.38$ minutes, so the time to the nearest minute is $13{:}03$, or $1{:}03$ pm.

Final answer

$(a)$ $D(2)=4.06\ldots$ m, just over $4$ m. $\quad (b)$ $13{:}03$, or $1{:}03$ pm.

8. WMA12/01 October 2025 · Q8

Medium8 marks · main topic: Geometric Sequences
Show worked solution
  1. Write S n

    $S_n=a+ar+ar^2+\cdots+ar^{n-1}$.

  2. Multiply by r

    $rS_n=ar+ar^2+ar^3+\cdots+ar^n$.

  3. Subtract

    $S_n-rS_n=a-ar^n=a(1-r^n)$.

  4. Rearrange

    $(1-r)S_n=a(1-r^n)$, so $S_n=\dfrac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r}$.

  5. Use the yearly multiplier

    An $8\%$ decrease each year means the multiplier is $0.92$.

  6. Show the amount after six years

    After $6$ years, the amount is $150(0.92)^6=90.953\ldots$, which is approximately $91$ litres.

  7. Set up the forty barrel total

    At the end of $40$ years, the amounts are $150(0.92),150(0.92)^2,\ldots,150(0.92)^{40}$.

  8. Sum the geometric sequence

    The total is $150(0.92)\dfrac{1-0.92^{40}}{1-0.92}=1663.581\ldots$.

  9. Round the total

    To the nearest litre, the total amount is $1664$ litres.

Final answer

$(i)$ Proven. $\quad (ii)(a)$ Approximately $91$ litres. $\quad (ii)(b)$ $1664$ litres.

Show worked solution
  1. Substitute the given information

    When $t=3$, $w=10$. So $10=\log_a(3+5)-\log_a4=\log_a8-\log_a4$.

  2. Combine the logarithms

    $\log_a8-\log_a4=\log_a\left(\dfrac84\right)=\log_a2$, so $10=\log_a2$.

  3. Find a

    $10=\log_a2$ means $a^{10}=2$, hence $a=2^{1/10}=1.071773\ldots=1.072$ to 3 decimal places.

  4. Start from the model

    Using $a=1.072$, $w=\log_{1.072}(t+5)-\log_{1.072}4$.

  5. Combine the logs again

    $w=\log_{1.072}\left(\dfrac{t+5}{4}\right)$.

  6. Make t the subject

    Changing from logarithmic form to exponential form gives $(1.072)^w=\dfrac{t+5}{4}$, so $t=4(1.072)^w-5$.

  7. Substitute w equals fifteen

    When $w=15$, $t=4(1.072)^{15}-5=6.3496\ldots$, so $t=6.35$ months to 3 significant figures.

Final answer

$(a)$ $a=1.072$ to 3 d.p. $\quad (b)\ t=4(1.072)^w-5$. $\quad (c)\ t=6.35$ months to 3 s.f.

Show worked solution
  1. Set up the growth ratio

    Let the yearly ratio be $r$. After 3 years, $30000r^3=34000$.

  2. Find p

    $r^3=\dfrac{17}{15}$, so $r=\left(\dfrac{17}{15}\right)^{1/3}\approx1.04257$. Therefore $p=(r-1)\times100\approx4.26$.

  3. Set up the inequality

    After $N$ years the model gives $30000r^N$. We need $30000r^N>75000$, so $r^N>2.5$.

  4. Take logarithms

    $N\log r>\log(2.5)$, so $N>\dfrac{\log(2.5)}{\log r}$.

  5. Find the smallest integer

    Using $r\approx1.04257$, $\dfrac{\log(2.5)}{\log r}\approx21.98$. Hence the smallest integer value is $N=22$.

Final answer

13. WMA12/01 May/June 2023 · Q6

Hard9 marks · main topic: Geometric Sequences
Show worked solution
  1. Use the geometric condition

    For three consecutive terms of a geometric sequence, the middle term squared equals the product of the outer terms.

  2. Set up the equation

    So $k^2=(2k-15)(k+4)$.

  3. Show the required quadratic

    Expanding gives $k^2=2k^2-7k-60$, hence $k^2-7k-60=0$.

  4. Find k

    $k^2-7k-60=(k-12)(k+5)=0$, so $k=12$ or $k=-5$. The download numbers must be positive, so $k=12$.

  5. Find the common ratio

    The first three monthly values are $9$, $12$ and $16$ thousand. The common ratio is $\dfrac{12}{9}=\dfrac43$.

  6. Predict month four

    The 4th month is $16\cdot\dfrac43=\dfrac{64}{3}$ thousand downloads, which is about $21300$ downloads.

  7. Write the cumulative total

    In thousands, the total after $N$ months is $S_N=\dfrac{9\left(\left(\dfrac43\right)^N-1\right)}{\dfrac43-1}=27\left(\left(\dfrac43\right)^N-1\right)$.

  8. Compare with three million

    Three million downloads is $3000$ thousand, so we need $27\left(\left(\dfrac43\right)^N-1\right)>3000$.

  9. Solve the inequality

    $\left(\dfrac43\right)^N>\dfrac{1009}{9}$. Taking logarithms gives $N>\dfrac{\log(1009/9)}{\log(4/3)}=16.405\ldots$.

  10. Choose the first month

    The first integer month for which the total exceeds $3$ million is $N=17$.

Final answer

$(a)$ Proven. $\quad (b)$ $\dfrac{64}{3}$ thousand downloads, about $21300$ downloads. $\quad (c)$ $N=17$.

  1. Use the arithmetic rule

    For the first $20$ hits, the additional distances form an arithmetic sequence with first term $100$ and common difference $-2$.

  2. Show the twentieth hit

    The twentieth additional distance is $100+19(-2)=62$ mm.

  3. Sum the first twenty hits

    $S_{20}=\dfrac{20}{2}(100+62)=1620$ mm.

  4. Use the geometric information

    After the twentieth hit, the distances follow the same ratio, so the twenty second distance is $62r^2$.

  5. Find r

    $62r^2=60$, hence $r=\sqrt{\dfrac{30}{31}}=0.984$ to 3 decimal places.

  6. Set up the remaining distance

    More than $3$ m means more than $3000$ mm. After $20$ hits, the remaining amount to exceed is $3000-1620=1380$ mm.

  7. Sum the geometric tail

    If there are $m$ further hits, their total is $62r+62r^2+\cdots+62r^m=62r\dfrac{1-r^m}{1-r}$.

  8. Find the first m that works

  9. State N

    Therefore $m=28$ further hits are needed after the first $20$, so $N=20+28=48$.

17. WMA12/01 January 2025 · Q3

Hard10 marks · main topic: Applications of Differentiation
Show worked solution
  1. Use the volume

    The volume is $(3x)(x)(y)=3x^2y$. Since the capacity is $120$, $3x^2y=120$, so $y=\dfrac{40}{x^2}$.

  2. Write the metal area

    The open top means the metal area is the base plus four sides: $A=3x^2+2(3xy)+2(xy)$.

  3. Substitute for y

    $A=3x^2+8xy=3x^2+8x\left(\dfrac{40}{x^2}\right)=3x^2+\dfrac{320}{x}$. Hence $P=3$ and $Q=320$.

  4. Differentiate

    $\dfrac{dA}{dx}=6x-320x^{-2}=6x-\dfrac{320}{x^2}$.

  5. Use the stationary condition

    Set $\dfrac{dA}{dx}=0$: $6x=\dfrac{320}{x^2}$, so $6x^3=320$.

  6. Find x

    $x^3=\dfrac{160}{3}$, so $x=\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{160}{3}}=3.764\ldots$. To $3$ significant figures, $x=3.76$.

  7. Differentiate again

    $\dfrac{d^2A}{dx^2}=6+640x^{-3}=6+\dfrac{640}{x^3}$.

  8. Show the minimum

    For $x>0$, $6+\dfrac{640}{x^3}>0$, so the stationary value found in part (b) is a minimum.

Final answer

$(a)$ $A=3x^2+\dfrac{320}{x}$, so $P=3$ and $Q=320$. $\quad (b)$ $x=3.76$. $\quad (c)$ $\dfrac{d^2A}{dx^2}=6+\dfrac{640}{x^3}$, minimum.

  1. Rewrite the trig equation

    $5\sin x\tan x+13=\cos x$ becomes $\dfrac{5\sin^2x}{\cos x}+13=\cos x$.

  5. Choose the valid root

    $\cos x=\dfrac52$ is impossible, so $\cos x=-\dfrac13$. For $0<x\le\pi$, $x=1.9106\ldots$, so $x=1.91$ radians to 3 significant figures.

  6. Use the temperature at 6 am

    At $t=6$, $20=10+12\sin(6k+18)^\circ$, so $\sin(6k+18)^\circ=\dfrac56$.

  7. Solve for k

    $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac56\right)=56.442\ldots^\circ$. Since $0<k<20$, $18<6k+18<138$, so $6k+18=56.442\ldots$ or $123.557\ldots$.

  8. Find possible k values

    This gives $k=6.407\ldots$ or $k=17.592\ldots$, so $k=6.41$ or $17.59$ to 2 decimal places.

  9. Use the tighter condition

    Given further that $0<k<10$, use $k=6.407\ldots$.

  10. Find the maximum temperature

    The maximum value of $\sin$ is $1$, so the maximum temperature is $10+12=22^\circ\text{C}$.

  11. Find the time of maximum

    The maximum occurs when $kt+18=90$, so $t=\dfrac{72}{6.407\ldots}=11.2375\ldots$ hours after midnight.

  12. Convert to time of day

    $0.2375\ldots$ hours is $14.25\ldots$ minutes, so the maximum occurs at about $11{:}14$ am.

Final answer

$(i)$ $x=1.91$ radians. $\quad (ii)(a)$ $k=6.41$ or $17.59$. $\quad (ii)(b)$ $22^\circ\text{C}$. $\quad (ii)(c)$ $11{:}14$ am.
