1. WMA12/01 May/June R 2024 · Q5

Medium6 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Substitute t equals two

    $D=8+5\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi(2)}{6}+3\right)=8+5\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}+3\right)$.

  2. Evaluate

    $D=4.064\ldots$, so the depth at 2 am is just over $4$ metres.

  3. Set depth equal to six

    $6=8+5\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{6}+3\right)$, so $\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{6}+3\right)=-\dfrac25$.

  4. Use the time interval

    After midday means $t>12$. Then $\dfrac{\pi t}{6}+3$ is greater than $2\pi+3$.

  5. Choose the first suitable angle

    The first angle after $2\pi+3$ with sine $-\dfrac25$ is $3\pi+\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac25\right)$.

  6. Solve for t

    $\dfrac{\pi t}{6}+3=3\pi+\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac25\right)$, so $t=13.056\ldots$ hours after midnight.

  7. Convert to clock time

    $0.056\ldots$ hours is about $3.38$ minutes, so the time to the nearest minute is $13{:}03$, or $1{:}03$ pm.

Final answer

$(a)$ $D(2)=4.06\ldots$ m, just over $4$ m. $\quad (b)$ $13{:}03$, or $1{:}03$ pm.

2. WMA12/01 May/June 2022 · Q5

Medium6 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Substitute u

    Let $u=\theta+43^\circ$. The equation becomes $3\tan u=2\cos u$.

  2. Clear the cosine denominator

    $3\dfrac{\sin u}{\cos u}=2\cos u$, so $3\sin u=2\cos^2u$.

  3. Use cos squared

    $3\sin u=2(1-\sin^2u)$, so $2\sin^2u+3\sin u-2=0$.

  4. Factor the quadratic

    $(2\sin u-1)(\sin u+2)=0$.

  5. Use the valid sine value

    $\sin u=-2$ is impossible, so $\sin u=\dfrac12$.

  6. Find u in the interval

    Since $-180^\circ<\theta\leq180^\circ$, $-137^\circ<u\leq223^\circ$. Thus $u=30^\circ$ or $150^\circ$.

  7. Return to theta

    $\theta=u-43^\circ$, so $\theta=-13^\circ$ or $107^\circ$.

Final answer

$\theta=-13^\circ,\ 107^\circ$.

  3. Use the identity

  5. Solve for sin theta

  6. Find the angle range

  7. Find the theta values

  8. Convert back to x

4. WMA12/01 May/June R 2024 · Q7

Medium7 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Rewrite tan x

    $3\sin x\tan x=11+\cos x$ becomes $\dfrac{3\sin^2x}{\cos x}=11+\cos x$.

  2. Multiply by cos x

    $3\sin^2x=11\cos x+\cos^2x$.

  3. Use sine squared

    $3(1-\cos^2x)=11\cos x+\cos^2x$, so $4\cos^2x+11\cos x-3=0$.

  4. Solve for cosine

    $(4\cos x-1)(\cos x+3)=0$, so $\cos x=\dfrac14$ or $\cos x=-3$.

  5. Reject impossible value

    $\cos x=-3$ is impossible, so $\cos x=\dfrac14$.

  6. Find x values

    For $0\le x<2\pi$, this gives $x=1.318\ldots$ or $x=4.965\ldots$, so $x=1.318,4.965$ radians.

  7. Use the right triangle identity

    Given $0<\theta<90^\circ$ and $\cos\theta=\dfrac13$, $\sin\theta=\sqrt{1-\dfrac19}=\dfrac{2\sqrt2}{3}$.

  8. Find tan theta

    $\tan\theta=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\dfrac{2\sqrt2/3}{1/3}=2\sqrt2$.

Final answer

$(i)$ $x=1.318,\ 4.965$. $\quad (ii)$ $2\sqrt2$.

7. WMA12/01 October 2022 · Q3

Medium7 marks · main topic: Sequences & Series
Show worked solution
  1. Find the first term

    $a_1=\cos^2\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)=\left(\dfrac12\right)^2=\dfrac14$.

  2. Find the second term

    $a_2=\cos^2\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right)=\left(-\dfrac12\right)^2=\dfrac14$.

  3. Find the third term

    $a_3=\cos^2(\pi)=(-1)^2=1$.

  4. Recognise the repeating pattern

    The values of $\cos^2\left(\dfrac{n\pi}{3}\right)$ repeat every 3 terms as $\dfrac14,\dfrac14,1$.

  5. Sum the n part

    $\sum_{n=1}^{50} n=\dfrac{50\cdot51}{2}=1275$.

  6. Sum the cosine part

    There are $16$ complete groups of 3 terms in the first $48$ terms, then the first 2 terms repeat. Hence the cosine-square sum is $16\left(\dfrac14+\dfrac14+1\right)+\dfrac14+\dfrac14=24+\dfrac12=\dfrac{49}{2}$.

  7. Add the two sums

    $\sum_{n=1}^{50}\left(n+\cos^2\left(\dfrac{n\pi}{3}\right)\right)=1275+\dfrac{49}{2}=\dfrac{2599}{2}$.

Final answer

$(a)\ a_1=\dfrac14,\ a_2=\dfrac14,\ a_3=1. $\quad $(b)\ \dfrac{2599}{2}$.

  4. Simplify

  5. Use part a

    The equation becomes $\sin x+\dfrac2{\sin x}=4\sin x-5$.

  6. Form a quadratic in sin x

  1. Find the second term

    The 2nd term of $(3+2x)^6$ is $\binom61 3^5(2x)=6\cdot243\cdot2x=2916x$.

  2. Find the third term

    The 3rd term is $\binom62 3^4(2x)^2=15\cdot81\cdot4x^2=4860x^2$.

  3. Find the fifth term

    The 5th term is $\binom64 3^2(2x)^4=15\cdot9\cdot16x^4=2160x^4$.

  4. Use the geometric condition

    For three consecutive terms in a geometric series, the middle term squared equals the product of the two outer terms.

  5. Set up the equation

    $(4860x^2)^2=(2916x)(2160x^4)$.

  6. Solve for x

    For the non-zero geometric case, divide by $x^4$: $4860^2=2916\cdot2160\,x$, so $x=\dfrac{4860^2}{2916\cdot2160}=\dfrac{15}{4}$.

  7. Use the sum to infinity

    In the different geometric series, first term $=\sin^2\theta$, common ratio $=2\cos\theta$, and $S_\infty=\dfrac85$.

  8. Form the equation

    $\dfrac{\sin^2\theta}{1-2\cos\theta}=\dfrac85$.

  9. Use sine squared

    $5\sin^2\theta=8(1-2\cos\theta)$. With $\sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta$, this becomes $5(1-\cos^2\theta)=8-16\cos\theta$.

  10. Show the required equation

    Rearranging gives $5\cos^2\theta-16\cos\theta+3=0$.

  11. Solve for cosine

    $5\cos^2\theta-16\cos\theta+3=0$ gives $\cos\theta=3$ or $\cos\theta=\dfrac15$. The possible value is $\cos\theta=\dfrac15$.

  12. Find the second term

    The 2nd term is first term times common ratio: $\sin^2\theta(2\cos\theta)=\left(1-\dfrac{1}{25}\right)\cdot\dfrac25=\dfrac{24}{25}\cdot\dfrac25=\dfrac{48}{125}$.
