1. WMA12/01 May/June R 2024 · Q3

Easy5 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Complete the square

    $x^2+y^2+8x-14y-79=0$ becomes $(x+4)^2-16+(y-7)^2-49-79=0$.

  2. Write standard form

    $(x+4)^2+(y-7)^2=144$.

  3. Read centre and radius

    The centre is $(-4,7)$ and the radius is $12$.

  4. Find distance from centre to origin

    The distance from $(-4,7)$ to $O(0,0)$ is $\sqrt{(-4)^2+7^2}=\sqrt{65}$.

  5. Use origin inside the circle

    Since $\sqrt{65}<12$, the origin is inside the circle.

  6. Find nearest distance to the circle

    The nearest point on the circle lies along the line from the centre to the origin, so $OP=12-\sqrt{65}$.

Final answer

$(a)(i)$ $(-4,7)$. $\quad (a)(ii)$ $12$. $\quad (b)$ $12-\sqrt{65}$.

2. WMA12/01 January 2026 · Q5

Easy6 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Complete the square

    $x^2-6x=(x-3)^2-9$ and $y^2+5y=\left(y+\dfrac52\right)^2-\dfrac{25}{4}$.

  2. Write the circle in centre-radius form

    The equation becomes $(x-3)^2+\left(y+\dfrac52\right)^2=\dfrac{225}{4}$.

  3. Read the centre and radius

    The centre is $\left(3,-\dfrac52\right)$ and the radius is $\sqrt{\dfrac{225}{4}}=\dfrac{15}{2}$.

  4. Use the touching condition

    The centre of $C_2$ is $(-k,0)$ and its radius is $5$. Since the circles touch externally, the distance between centres is $\dfrac{15}{2}+5=\dfrac{25}{2}$.

  5. Solve for k

    $(k+3)^2+\left(\dfrac52\right)^2=\left(\dfrac{25}{2}\right)^2$, so $(k+3)^2=150$. Since $k>0$, $k+3=5\sqrt6$, hence $k=5\sqrt6-3$.

Final answer

$(a)(i)\ (3,-\dfrac52).\quad (a)(ii)\ \dfrac{15}{2}.\quad (b)\ k=5\sqrt6-3$.

3. WMA12/01 May/June 2024 · Q7

Show worked solution
  1. Complete the square

    $x^2+y^2+8x-10y=29$ becomes $(x+4)^2-16+(y-5)^2-25=29$.

  2. Write circle C1 in standard form

    $(x+4)^2+(y-5)^2=70$.

  3. Read centre and radius

    The centre of $C_1$ is $(-4,5)$ and its radius is $\sqrt{70}$.

  4. Read C2

    $C_2$ has equation $(x-5)^2+(y+8)^2=52$, so its centre is $(5,-8)$ and its radius is $\sqrt{52}=2\sqrt{13}$.

  5. Find distance between centres

    The distance between $(-4,5)$ and $(5,-8)$ is $\sqrt{9^2+(-13)^2}=\sqrt{250}=5\sqrt{10}$.

  6. Compare with radii

    $\sqrt{70}+2\sqrt{13}=15.58\ldots$, while $5\sqrt{10}=15.81\ldots$.

  7. Conclude

    The distance between centres is greater than the sum of the radii, so the circles are separate. Therefore they neither touch nor intersect.

Final answer

$(a)(i)$ $(-4,5)$. $\quad (a)(ii)$ $\sqrt{70}$. $\quad (b)$ The circles neither touch nor intersect.

  2. Write the circle equation

    Hence $C$ has equation $(x-3)^2+(y-5)^2=169$.

7. WMA12/01 May/June 2022 · Q3

Medium7 marks · main topic: Proof
Show worked solution
  1. Choose a counterexample

    Take $n=5$.

  2. Evaluate the expression

    $(5+1)^3-5^3=216-125=91$.

  3. Show it is not prime

    $91=7\times13$, so the statement is false.

  4. Find the gradient CA

    For $A(1,0)$ and $C(7,-6)$, the gradient of $CA$ is $\dfrac{0-(-6)}{1-7}=-1$.

  5. Find the gradient CB

    For $B(3,-10)$ and $C(7,-6)$, the gradient of $CB$ is $\dfrac{-10-(-6)}{3-7}=1$.

  6. Show the right angle

    The product of the gradients is $-1$, so $CA$ is perpendicular to $CB$ and $\angle ACB=90^\circ$.

  7. Conclude the diameter

    An angle of $90^\circ$ at the circumference subtends a diameter, so $AB$ is a diameter of the circle.

Final answer

$(i)$ Counterexample: $n=5$ gives $91$, not prime. $\quad (ii)$ Proven: $AB$ is a diameter.

  1. Complete the square

    $x^2-6x=(x-3)^2-9$ and $y^2+8y=(y+4)^2-16$.

  2. Write centre-radius form

    The circle becomes $(x-3)^2+(y+4)^2=25-k$.

  3. Use the tangent condition

    The centre is $(3,-4)$. Since the $x$-axis is tangent, the radius is the distance from the centre to the $x$-axis, which is $4$.

  4. Find k

    Thus $25-k=4^2=16$, so $k=9$.

  5. Find the intercepts

    With $k=9$, the circle is $(x-3)^2+(y+4)^2=16$. On the $x$-axis, $y=0$, giving $x=3$.

  6. Find the y-axis points

    On the $y$-axis, $x=0$, so $9+(y+4)^2=16$. Hence $y=-4\pm\sqrt7$.

  7. Calculate the triangle area

    The vertical base on the $y$-axis has length $2\sqrt7$, and its perpendicular distance to $(3,0)$ is $3$. The area is $\dfrac12(2\sqrt7)(3)=3\sqrt7$.

Final answer

$(a)\ k=9.\quad (b)\ 3\sqrt7$.

  1. Write the circle equation

    The circle has centre $(3,5)$ and radius $r$, so $(x-3)^2+(y-5)^2=r^2$.

  2. Substitute the line

    On the line, $y=2x+k$. Substitution gives $(x-3)^2+(2x+k-5)^2=r^2$.

  3. Expand

    $x^2-6x+9+4x^2+4kx-20x+k^2-10k+25-r^2=0$.

  4. Show the intersection equation

    Collecting terms gives $5x^2+(4k-26)x+k^2-10k+34-r^2=0$.

  5. Use tangency

    For a tangent, the quadratic has one repeated root, so its discriminant is zero.

  6. Set the discriminant to zero

    $(4k-26)^2-20(k^2-10k+34-r^2)=0$.

  7. Simplify

    This reduces to $20r^2-4k^2-8k-4=0$, hence $5r^2=(k+1)^2$. Therefore $p=1$.

  8. Find the tangent point x coordinate

    For the repeated root, $x_B=-\dfrac{4k-26}{2\cdot5}=\dfrac{13-2k}{5}$.

  9. Use distance AB

    The point $A$ is $(0,k)$ and $B$ lies on a line of gradient $2$, so $AB=\sqrt{x_B^2+(2x_B)^2}=\sqrt5\,x_B$.

  10. Use the radius relation

    From $5r^2=(k+1)^2$, $r=\dfrac{k+1}{\sqrt5}$ in the shown configuration.
