    \paragraph{Solution} Differentiate $x=e^{2\tan y}$ with respect to $y$:

    \[\frac{dx}{dy}=2\sec^2 y\,e^{2\tan y}=2x\sec^2y.\]

    This is positive on the stated interval, so

    \[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac1{2x\sec^2y}.\]

    Taking logarithms of the original relation gives $\ln x=2\tan y$. Using $\sec^2y=1+\tan^2y$,

    \[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac1{2x\left(1+\frac{(\ln x)^2}{4}\right)} =\frac2{x\bigl(4+(\ln x)^2\bigr)}.\]

    Hence $a=2$, $b=4$ and $n=2$.

Final answer

(main) $a=2,\ b=4,\ n=2$

    \paragraph{Solution} Put $u=x^2+(3k-2)x$, so $u'=2x+3k-2$. By the product and chain rules,

    \[\frac{dy}{dx}=2e^u+2xe^u(2x+3k-2) =2e^u\bigl(2x^2+(3k-2)x+1\bigr).\]

    As $2e^u\gt 0$, stationary points satisfy

    \[2x^2+(3k-2)x+1=0.\]

    For two distinct real roots, the discriminant must be positive:

    \[(3k-2)^2-8\gt 0 \Longleftrightarrow |3k-2|\gt 2\sqrt2.\]

    Consequently

    \[k\lt \frac{2-2\sqrt2}{3}\quad\hbox{or}\quad k\gt \frac{2+2\sqrt2}{3}.\]

    For these values the quadratic changes sign at both simple roots, so both stationary points are genuine turning points. Equality gives a repeated root with no derivative sign change and is excluded.

Final answer

(main) $(-infinity,(2-2*sqrt(2))/3) union ((2+2*sqrt(2))/3,infinity)$

  1. Part (a)

    Set $u=x^2+k\gt 0$. Applying the quotient and chain rules,

    \[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{(2x/u)u-2x\ln u}{u^2} =\frac{2x(1-\ln(x^2+k))}{(x^2+k)^2}.\]

    Comparing with the required form identifies both constants.

    \[\boxed{A=2,\qquad B=1}\]
  2. Part (b)

    The denominator is positive, so stationary points satisfy

    \[x=0\quad\text{or}\quad \ln(x^2+k)=1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=0\quad\text{or}\quad x=\pm\sqrt{e-k}.\]

    There are three distinct real stationary points precisely when $e-k\gt 0$. In that case the factor $1-\ln(x^2+k)$ is positive between the outer roots and negative outside them; $2x$ changes sign at zero. Thus the derivative changes sign at all three points, so all are turning points. Together with $k\gt 0$, the three coordinates below are real and distinct; the restriction is $0\lt k\lt e$.

    \[\boxed{x=0,\quad x=-\sqrt{e-k},\quad x=\sqrt{e-k}}\]
  3. Part (c)

    At $k=e$, the two outer stationary points merge with $x=0$, so there are no longer three distinct turning points. Values of $k$ can approach $e$ from below, but cannot equal it under this condition.
