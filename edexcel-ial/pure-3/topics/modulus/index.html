1. WMA13/01 January 2024 · Q1

Easy4 marks · main topic: Graph Transformations
Show worked solution
  1. Part (a)

    For $y=f(2x)$ the horizontal coordinate is divided by 2 while the vertical coordinate is unchanged: $(-4,-3)\mapsto(-4/2,-3)$.

    \[\boxed{(-2,-3)}\]
  2. Part (b)

    The inside shift moves the point right by 1; the outside factor multiplies its ordinate by 3. Thus $(-4,-3)\mapsto(-4+1,3(-3))$.

    \[\boxed{(-3,-9)}\]
  3. Part (c)

    Output modulus retains the input and replaces the output by its absolute value: $(-4,-3)\mapsto(-4,|-3|)$.

    \[\boxed{(-4,3)}\]
Final answer

(a) $(-2,-3)$ (b) $(-3,-9)$ (c) $(-4,3)$

2. WMA13/01 May/June 2024 · Q1

Medium6 marks

  1. Part (a)

    The modulus is smallest when $x-5=0$. At $x=5$, $f(5)=10$.

    \[\boxed{P=(5,10)}\]
  2. Part (b)

    For $x\lt 5$, $|x-5|=5-x$, so

    \[20-2x\gt 6x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\lt \frac52.\]

    This is consistent with $x\lt 5$. For $x\ge5$, $|x-5|=x-5$, so $2x\gt 6x$, which requires $x\lt 0$ and is inconsistent with this branch. The equality point $x=5/2$ is excluded because the inequality is strict.

    \[\boxed{x\lt \frac52}\]
  3. Part (c)

    The transformation $y=3f(x-2)$ moves a point $(x,y)$ to $(x+2,3y)$. Thus $P=(5,10)$ moves to $(5+2,3\cdot10)$.

    \[\boxed{(7,30)}\]
Final answer

(a) $P=(5,10)$ (b) $x\lt \frac52$ (c) $(7,30)$

3. WMA13/01 October 2025 · Q7

Medium7 marks · main topic: Applications of Differentiation
Show worked solution
  1. Solution

    \paragraph{Solution} Put $u=x^2+(3k-2)x$, so $u'=2x+3k-2$. By the product and chain rules,

    \[\frac{dy}{dx}=2e^u+2xe^u(2x+3k-2) =2e^u\bigl(2x^2+(3k-2)x+1\bigr).\]

    As $2e^u\gt 0$, stationary points satisfy

    \[2x^2+(3k-2)x+1=0.\]

    For two distinct real roots, the discriminant must be positive:

    \[(3k-2)^2-8\gt 0 \Longleftrightarrow |3k-2|\gt 2\sqrt2.\]

    Consequently

    \[k\lt \frac{2-2\sqrt2}{3}\quad\hbox{or}\quad k\gt \frac{2+2\sqrt2}{3}.\]

    For these values the quadratic changes sign at both simple roots, so both stationary points are genuine turning points. Equality gives a repeated root with no derivative sign change and is excluded.

Final answer

(main) $(-infinity,(2-2*sqrt(2))/3) union ((2+2*sqrt(2))/3,infinity)$

4. WMA13/01 October 2024 · Q3

  1. Part (a)

    Set $u=|x|\ge0$. Then

    \[2u^2-10u=48\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u^2-5u-24=(u-8)(u+3)=0.\]

    Reject $u=-3$ since $u\ge0$. Therefore $|x|=8$.

    \[\boxed{x=-8\ \text{or}\ x=8}\]
  2. Part (b)

    For $x\le0$, the right side $\frac52x\le0$ and the left side is nonnegative, so every such $x$ satisfies the inequality. For $0\lt x\lt 5$, $f(x)\lt 0$ and

    \[10x-2x^2\ge\frac52x \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\left(\frac{15}{2}-2x\right)\ge0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0\lt x\le\frac{15}{4}.\]

    For $x\ge5$, $f(x)\ge0$ and

    \[2x^2-10x\ge\frac52x \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\left(2x-\frac{25}{2}\right)\ge0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\ge\frac{25}{4}.\]

    Combining the cases retains equality at both boundary points.

    \[\boxed{x\le\frac{15}{4}\ \text{or}\ x\ge\frac{25}{4}}\]
Final answer

(a) $x=-8\ \text{or}\ x=8$ (b) $x\le\frac{15}{4}\ \text{or}\ x\ge\frac{25}{4}$

5. WMA13/01 October 2022 · Q2

Medium7 marks · main topic: Functions, Composition & Inverses
Show worked solution
  1. Part (a)

    For $x\ge0$, $0\lt 4/(3x+2)\le2$. Thus $f(0)=3$ is attained, while $f(x)$ increases towards 5 without reaching it.

    \[\boxed{3\le f(x)\lt 5}\]
  2. Part (b)

    (i) Put $y=5-4/(3x+2)$. Rearranging gives $(5-y)(3x+2)=4$, hence

    \[x=\frac{4/(5-y)-2}{3}=\frac{2y-6}{15-3y}.\]

    Exchange variables. (ii) The inverse domain is the range of $f$ found above.

    \[\boxed{f^{-1}(x)=\frac{2x-6}{15-3x},\qquad3\le x\lt 5}\]
  3. Part (c)

    The original function has an absolute value and angle $x/3+\pi/6$:

    \[g(-\pi)=|4\sin(-\pi/3+\pi/6)|=|4\sin(-\pi/6)|=2.\]

    Then $fg(-\pi)=f(2)=5-4/(3\cdot2+2)=5-1/2=9/2$.

    \[\boxed{fg(-\pi)=\frac92}\]
Final answer

(a) $3\le f(x)\lt 5$ (b) $f^{-1}(x)=\frac{2x-6}{15-3x},\qquad3\le x\lt 5$ (c) $fg(-\pi)=\frac92$

  1. Part (a)(i)

    For $x\le a/3$, $y=a-3x$; for $x\ge a/3$, $y=3x-a$. These two straight branches form a V with minimum $(a/3,0)$ and $y$-intercept $(0,a)$.

    \[\boxed{\text{minimum }(a/3,0),\quad y\text{-intercept }(0,a)}\]
  2. Part (a)(ii)

    Subtracting $b$ translates the previous graph vertically down by $b$. The minimum becomes $(a/3,-b)$ and the $y$-intercept becomes $(0,a-b)$, which is above the $x$-axis because $a\gt b\gt 0$.

    \[\boxed{\text{minimum }(a/3,-b),\quad y\text{-intercept }(0,a-b)}\]
  3. Part (b)

    For $x\le a/3$, the equation becomes $a-3x-b=5x$, giving $x=(a-b)/8$. This is positive and less than $a/8\lt a/3$, so it satisfies this branch condition. For $x\ge a/3$, the equation becomes $3x-a-b=5x$, giving $x=-(a+b)/2\lt 0$. This contradicts $x\ge a/3\gt 0$ and must be rejected. Therefore only one solution remains.

    \[\boxed{x=\frac{a-b}{8}}\]
Final answer

(a)(i) $\text{minimum }(a/3,0),\quad y\text{-intercept }(0,a)$ (a)(ii) $\text{minimum }(a/3,-b),\quad y\text{-intercept }(0,a-b)$ (b) $x=\frac{a-b}{8}$

7. WMA13/01 January 2023 · Q6

  1. Part (a)

    For $y=|3x-5a|-2a$ with $a\gt 0$: (i) At $x=0$, $y=5a-2a=3a$, so $P=(0,3a)$. (ii) At $y=0$, $|3x-5a|=2a$ gives $3x-5a=\pm2a$. Thus $x=a$ or $7a/3$; since $a\lt 7a/3$, $Q=(a,0)$ and $R=(7a/3,0)$. (iii) The modulus vanishes at $x=5a/3$, giving the minimum $S=(5a/3,-2a)$.

    \[\boxed{P=(0,3a),\quad Q=(a,0),\quad R=(7a/3,0),\quad S=(5a/3,-2a)}\]
  2. Part (b)

    Split $|3x-5a|-2a=|x-2a|$ at $x=5a/3$ and $x=2a$; their order uses $a\gt 0$. For $x\lt 5a/3$: $3a-3x=2a-x$, so $x=a/2$, which is in this region. For $5a/3\le x\lt 2a$: $3x-7a=2a-x$, so $x=9a/4$; this is outside the assumed region and is rejected. For $x\ge2a$: $3x-7a=x-2a$, so $x=5a/2$, which is valid. These three regions cover the whole real line, so there are exactly two solutions.

    \[\boxed{x=\frac a2,\qquad x=\frac{5a}2}\]
Final answer

(a) $P=(0,3a),\quad Q=(a,0),\quad R=(7a/3,0),\quad S=(5a/3,-2a)$ (b) $x=\frac a2,\qquad x=\frac{5a}2$

8. WMA13/01 May/June 2022 · Q5

  1. Part (a)

    (i) At $x=0$, $f(0)=|-9|-2=7$, so the y coordinate of A is 7. (ii) The minimum occurs when the modulus vanishes: $kx-9=0$, hence $x_B=9/k$.

    \[\boxed{y_A=7,\qquad x_B=\frac9k}\]
  2. Part (b)

    $|kx-9|-2\lt 0$ means $|kx-9|\lt 2$, or $-2\lt kx-9\lt 2$. Add 9 and divide by the positive k, preserving both strict inequality signs.

    \[\boxed{\frac7k\lt x\lt \frac{11}k}\]
  3. Part (c)

    For $x\le9/k$, the graph is $y=7-kx$; for $x\ge9/k$, it is $y=kx-11$. The intersection of the right branch with $3-2x$ has $x=14/(k+2)$. For it to lie strictly to the right of the vertex,

    \[\frac{14}{k+2}\gt \frac9k\ \Longleftrightarrow\ 14k\gt 9k+18 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ k\gt 18/5.\]

    For these k, the left-branch intersection has $x=4/(k-2)\lt 9/k$, so both intersections exist and differ. At $k=18/5$ the two points coincide at the vertex. Each straight branch can supply at most one intersection; when the right branch is inadmissible, two distinct points cannot occur (k=2 gives parallel, unequal left lines).

    \[\boxed{k\gt \frac{18}{5}}\]
Final answer

(a) $y_A=7,\qquad x_B=\frac9k$ (b) $\frac7k\lt x\lt \frac{11}k$ (c) $k\gt \frac{18}{5}$

  1. Part (a)

    Apply f twice, in order: $f(6)=21-2|2-6|=13$. Then $ff(6)=f(13)=21-2|2-13|=21-22=-1$. The intermediate input 13 lies in the domain $x\ge0$.

    \[\boxed{ff(6)=-1}\]
  2. Part (b)

    For $0\le x\le2$, $f(x)=17+2x$, so $17+2x=5x$ gives $x=17/3$, outside that branch; reject it. For $x\ge2$, $f(x)=25-2x$. Then $25-2x=5x$, giving $x=25/7$. This belongs to the second branch and satisfies the original modulus equation.

    \[\boxed{x=\frac{25}{7}}\]
  3. Part (c)

    On the left branch $0\le x\le2$, f rises from $f(0)=17$ to $f(2)=21$. The right branch decreases from 21 without lower bound. A horizontal line therefore meets both branches at distinct points exactly when $17\le k\lt 21$. At $k=17$, one root is the permitted endpoint $x=0$; at $k=21$, the two intersections merge at the vertex, so 21 is excluded.

    \[\boxed{17\le k\lt 21}\]
  4. Part (d)

    The original vertex is $(2,21)$. Under $y=af(x-b)$ it maps to $(2+b,21a)$. Comparing with $(6,3)$ gives $2+b=6$ and $21a=3$.

    \[\boxed{a=\frac17,\qquad b=4}\]

11. WMA13/01 October 2023 · Q9

Hard9 marks · main topic: Exponentials & Logarithms
Show worked solution
  1. Part (a)

    The logarithm requires $x+1\gt 0$, so the domain is $x\gt -1$. As $x\to-1^+$, $\ln(x+1)\to-\infty$ and the ordinate tends to infinity.

    \[\boxed{k=-1}\]
  2. Part (b)

    (i) At the $y$-axis, $x=0$, giving $y=|2-4\ln1|=2$.

    (ii) At the $x$-axis, $|2-4\ln(x+1)|=0$, so

    \[\ln(x+1)=\frac12\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=e^{1/2}-1.\]

    Thus $A=(0,2)$ and $B=(e^{1/2}-1,0)$.

    \[\boxed{y_A=2,\qquad x_B=e^{1/2}-1}\]
  3. Part (c)

    On $x\gt -1$, split the absolute-value inequality into two alternatives:

    \[2-4\ln(x+1)\gt 3\quad\text{or}\quad 2-4\ln(x+1)\lt -3.\]

    Dividing by $-4$ reverses the inequality in each branch:

    \[\ln(x+1)\lt -\frac14\quad\text{or}\quad \ln(x+1)\gt \frac54.\]

    Since the exponential is increasing, this becomes $x+1\lt e^{-1/4}$ or $x+1\gt e^{5/4}$. Intersect with the logarithmic domain; neither boundary is included because the source inequality is strict.

    \[\boxed{-1\lt x\lt e^{-1/4}-1\quad\text{or}\quad x\gt e^{5/4}-1}\]
Final answer

(a) $k=-1$ (b) $y_A=2,\qquad x_B=e^{1/2}-1$ (c) $-1\lt x\lt e^{-1/4}-1\quad\text{or}\quad x\gt e^{5/4}-1$

14. WMA13/01 January 2020 · Q6

Show worked solution
  1. Part (a)

    At the vertex, the expression inside the modulus is zero:

    \[2x-5=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\frac52,\qquad f\!\left(\frac52\right)=3.\]

    Hence \(\boxed{P=\left(\frac52,3\right)}\).

  2. Part (b)

    Write the function in its two branches:

    \[f(x)=\begin{cases} 13-4x,&0\leq x\leq\frac52,\\ 4x-7,&x\geq\frac52. \end{cases}\]

    On the left branch,

    \[13-4x=3x-2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 7x=15 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\frac{15}{7}.\]

    This lies in \(0\leq x\leq\frac52\), so it is valid. On the right branch,

    \[4x-7=3x-2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=5.\]

    This satisfies \(x\geq\frac52\), so it is also valid. Thus

    \[\boxed{x=\frac{15}{7}\ \text{or}\ x=5}.\]
  3. Part (c)

    Each straight branch can have at most one intersection with \(y=kx+2\). Two distinct roots therefore require one intersection strictly to each side of the vertex.

    For the left branch,

    \[13-4x=kx+2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_L=\frac{11}{k+4}.\]

    The condition \(0\leq x_L\lt \frac52\) first requires \(k+4\gt 0\), then gives

    \[\frac{11}{k+4}\lt \frac52 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 22\lt 5(k+4) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k\gt \frac25.\]

    For the right branch,

    \[4x-7=kx+2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_R=\frac{9}{4-k}.\]

    The condition \(x_R\gt \frac52\) requires \(k\lt 4\), and then

    \[\frac{9}{4-k}\gt \frac52 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 18\gt 5(4-k) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k\gt \frac25.\]

    At \(k=\frac25\), both intersections coincide at the vertex, giving only one root. At \(k=4\), the line is parallel to the right branch and has no intersection with that branch, again leaving only one root. Hence

    \[\boxed{\frac25\lt k\lt 4}.\]
Final answer

(a) $P=\left(\frac52,3\right)$ (b) $x=\frac{15}{7}\ \text{or}\ x=5$ (c) $\frac25\lt k\lt 4$
