  1. Solution

    \paragraph{Solution} Differentiate $x=e^{2\tan y}$ with respect to $y$:

    \[\frac{dx}{dy}=2\sec^2 y\,e^{2\tan y}=2x\sec^2y.\]

    This is positive on the stated interval, so

    \[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac1{2x\sec^2y}.\]

    Taking logarithms of the original relation gives $\ln x=2\tan y$. Using $\sec^2y=1+\tan^2y$,

    \[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac1{2x\left(1+\frac{(\ln x)^2}{4}\right)} =\frac2{x\bigl(4+(\ln x)^2\bigr)}.\]

    Hence $a=2$, $b=4$ and $n=2$.

Final answer

(main) $a=2,\ b=4,\ n=2$
