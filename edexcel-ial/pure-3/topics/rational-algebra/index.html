  1. Solution

    \paragraph{Solution} Put $u=x^2+(3k-2)x$, so $u'=2x+3k-2$. By the product and chain rules,

    \[\frac{dy}{dx}=2e^u+2xe^u(2x+3k-2) =2e^u\bigl(2x^2+(3k-2)x+1\bigr).\]

    As $2e^u\gt 0$, stationary points satisfy

    \[2x^2+(3k-2)x+1=0.\]

    For two distinct real roots, the discriminant must be positive:

    \[(3k-2)^2-8\gt 0 \Longleftrightarrow |3k-2|\gt 2\sqrt2.\]

    Consequently

    \[k\lt \frac{2-2\sqrt2}{3}\quad\hbox{or}\quad k\gt \frac{2+2\sqrt2}{3}.\]

    For these values the quadratic changes sign at both simple roots, so both stationary points are genuine turning points. Equality gives a repeated root with no derivative sign change and is excluded.

Final answer

(main) $(-infinity,(2-2*sqrt(2))/3) union ((2+2*sqrt(2))/3,infinity)$
