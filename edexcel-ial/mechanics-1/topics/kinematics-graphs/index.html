1. WME01/01 October 2021 · Q6

Easy5 marks · main topic: Constant Acceleration in 1D
Show worked solution
  1. Use the velocity-time graph

    The velocity becomes zero at $t=2.5$ s. Since acceleration is $-g=-9.8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, the initial velocity is $u=9.8(2.5)$.

  2. Find the initial speed

    $u=24.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

  3. Use displacement to the ground

    From launch to $t=7$, the displacement is the area under the velocity-time graph. Algebraically, $s=ut-\dfrac12gt^2$ with $t=7$.

  4. Calculate displacement

    $s=24.5(7)-\dfrac12(9.8)(7^2)=171.5-240.1=-68.6$ m.

  5. State the height

    The negative sign shows the ground is below A, so the height of A above the ground is $68.6\ \mathrm{m}$.

Final answer

$(a)\ 24.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.\quad (b)\ 68.6\ \mathrm{m}\ \text{approximately}$.

2. WME01/01 January 2022 · Q4

Medium8 marks · main topic: Constant Acceleration in 1D
Show worked solution
  1. Find the initial speed

    At the highest point B, the velocity is $0$ and $AB=19.6$ m. Use $v^2=u^2+2as$ upward: $0=u^2-2g(19.6)$.

  2. Calculate u

    $u^2=2(9.8)(19.6)=384.16$, so $u=19.6\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

  3. Use displacement to the ground

    From A to the ground, taking upward as positive, displacement is $-24.5$ m. Use $s=ut+\dfrac12at^2$: $-24.5=19.6T-4.9T^2$.

  4. Solve for T

    $4.9T^2-19.6T-24.5=0$. Divide by $4.9$: $T^2-4T-5=0$, so $(T-5)(T+1)=0$. The positive time is $T=5$ s.

  5. Describe the speed-time graph

    The speed starts at $19.6$, decreases linearly to $0$ at $t=2$, then increases linearly until the ball hits the ground at $t=5$. The final speed is $29.4$, so the second line is longer than the first.

Final answer

$(a)\ T=5\ \mathrm{s}.\quad (b)$ Speed-time graph is V-shaped, from speed $19.6$ at $t=0$ to $0$ at $t=2$, then to speed $29.4$ at $t=5$.

  1. Use area under the speed-time graph

    Distance is the area under a speed-time graph. The three areas are a triangle from $0$ to $12$, a rectangle from $12$ to $31$, and a trapezium from $31$ to $41$.

  2. Show that U is 6

    $\dfrac12(12)(3U)+19(3U)+\dfrac12(3U+2U)(10)=600$. This gives $18U+57U+25U=600$, so $100U=600$ and $U=6$.

  3. Find the distance to speed 6

    Since $U=6$, the final speed $6\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ occurs during the first section. The acceleration there is $\dfrac{18-0}{12}=1.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, so the time to reach $6$ is $4$ s. The distance is $\dfrac12(4)(6)=12\ \mathrm{m}$.

  4. Find the acceleration and deceleration

    From $0$ to $12$, $a=\dfrac{18}{12}=1.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. From $31$ to $41$, the speed falls from $18$ to $12$, so the deceleration is $\dfrac{18-12}{10}=0.6\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

  5. Describe the acceleration-time graph

    The acceleration-time graph has three horizontal sections: $1.5$ from $0\leq t\leq 12$, $0$ from $12<t\leq31$, and $-0.6$ from $31<t\leq41$.

Final answer

$(a)\ U=6.\quad (b)\ 12\ \mathrm{m}.\quad (c)\ 1.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}.\quad (d)\ 0.6\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}.\quad (e)\ a(t)=1.5,0,-0.6$ on the three intervals.

5. WME01/01 October 2025 · Q6

Hard10 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Part (a): Find accelerations

    (i) Acceleration for $0 \leq t \leq 10$ $\text{Acceleration} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \text{gradient of v-t graph}$ $a = \frac{30 - 0}{10 - 0}$ $a = 3 \text{ m s}^{-2}$ (ii) Deceleration for $30 \leq t \leq 35$ $a = \frac{0 - 30}{35 - 30}$ $a = \frac{-30}{5}$ $a = -6 \text{ m s}^{-2}$ Deceleration = $|-6| = 6$ m s$^{-2}$

  2. Part (b): Sketch acceleration-time graph

    Analysis of each section: ; $0 \leq t \leq 10$: Constant acceleration = 3 m s$^{-2}$ ; $10 < t \leq 30$: Constant velocity, so acceleration = 0 m s$^{-2}$ ; $30 < t \leq 35$: Constant deceleration = $-6$ m s$^{-2}$

  3. Part (c): Find distance travelled for $0 \leq t \leq 35$ - Calculate area under v-t graph

    $\text{Distance} = \text{Area under v-t graph}$ The shape is a trapezium. We can split it into regions: Method 1: Trapezium formula $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h$ Where the parallel sides are the time intervals and height is velocity. Better to calculate as sum of shapes: Method 2: Sum of triangle + rectangle + triangle $\text{Area}_1 = \frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 30 = 150 \text{ m}$ $\text{Area}_2 = 20 \times 30 = 600 \text{ m}$ $\text{Area}_3 = \frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 30 = 75 \text{ m}$ $\text{Total distance} = 150 + 600 + 75$ $= 825 \text{ m}$

  4. Part (d): Find value of A - Distance travelled by car (0 to 20 seconds)

    Area under v-t graph from $t = 0$ to $t = 20$: $\text{Distance}_{\text{car}} = \text{Triangle} + \text{Rectangle}$ $= \frac{1}{2}(10)(30) + (10)(30)$ $= 150 + 300$ $= 450 \text{ m}$

  5. Part (d): Find value of A - Distance travelled by motorcycle (5 to 20 seconds)

    Motorcycle starts at $t = 5$ with constant acceleration $A$. Time of travel: $20 - 5 = 15$ seconds $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ $\text{Distance}_{\text{motorcycle}} = 0 + \frac{1}{2}A(15)^2$ $= \frac{225A}{2}$ $= 112.5A \text{ m}$

  6. Part (d): Find value of A - Set distances equal

    Motorcycle catches up with car when $t = 20$: $112.5A = 450$ $A = \frac{450}{112.5}$ $A = 4 \text{ m s}^{-2}$

Final answer

$(a)(i)\ a=3\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}},\ (ii)\ \text{deceleration}=6\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}.\quad (b)\ \text{correct acceleration-time graph with levels }3,0,-6.\quad (c)\ \text{distance}=825\ \mathrm{m}.\quad (d)\ A=4\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

    $AB=28+36+120=184\ \mathrm{m}$.

  1. Describe the graph

    The car's graph is a horizontal line at $40\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The motorcyclist's graph starts at zero, rises with gradient $4$ until $t=15$, then rises with smaller gradient $1$ for a further $T$ seconds.

  2. Find the equal-speed time

    Before $t=15$, the motorcyclist's speed is $4t$. Set this equal to the car's speed: $4t_1=40$, so $t_1=10$ s.

  3. Find the motorcyclist's speed at 15 seconds

    After the first $15$ seconds, the motorcyclist's speed is $4(15)=60\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

  4. Find the final speed at catch-up

    During the next $T$ seconds the acceleration is $1\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, so the speed at catch-up is $60+T$.

  5. Equate distances

    Motorcyclist distance $=\dfrac12(15)(60)+\dfrac12[60+(60+T)]T$. Car distance $=40(15+T)$.

  6. Simplify the equation

    $450+60T+\dfrac12T^2=600+40T$. Hence $\dfrac12T^2+20T-150=0$, so $T^2+40T-300=0$ and $k=40$.

Final answer

$(a)$ Car: horizontal line at $40$; motorcyclist: two rising straight-line sections ending at the catch-up time. $(b)\ t_1=10\ \mathrm{s}.\quad (c)\ T^2+40T-300=0$, so $k=40$.

  1. Sketch the speed-time graph

    The graph starts at speed $5$ when $t=0$, rises linearly to speed $V$ at time $T_1$, then falls linearly to speed $0$ at time $T_1+T_2$.

  2. Use area from A to B

    The area under the first section is the distance $AB=132$. Thus $\dfrac12(5+V)T_1=132$, so $T_1=\dfrac{264}{V+5}$.

  3. Use area from B to C

    The second section is a triangle of height $V$ and base $T_2$. Since $BC=136$, $\dfrac12VT_2=136$, so $T_2=\dfrac{272}{V}$.

  4. Use the total time

    The whole journey takes $28$ s, so $\dfrac{264}{V+5}+\dfrac{272}{V}=28$.

  5. Solve for V

    Multiplying by $V(V+5)$ gives $264V+272(V+5)=28V(V+5)$. This simplifies to $7V^2-99V-340=0$, so $(V-17)(7V+20)=0$. Since speed is positive, $V=17$.

  6. Find the deceleration

    For the final section, $T_2=\dfrac{272}{17}=16$ s. The speed falls from $17$ to $0$, so the deceleration is $\dfrac{17}{16}=1.0625\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

Final answer

$(b)(i)\ T_1=\dfrac{264}{V+5}.\quad (b)(ii)\ T_2=\dfrac{272}{V}.\quad (c)\ V=17.\quad (d)\ \text{deceleration}=\dfrac{17}{16}=1.0625\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

  1. Find the car distance

    The car graph is a triangle from $0$ to $30$ plus a rectangle from $30$ to $T$. Distance $=\dfrac12(30)V+V(T-30)$.

  2. Simplify the car distance

    $\dfrac12(30)V+V(T-30)=15V+VT-30V=V(T-15)$, as required.

  3. Describe the motorbike graph

    The motorbike starts at $(10,0)$, accelerates with the same gradient as the car until $t=50$, then travels at constant speed until $t=60$, then decelerates linearly to $(T,V)$.

  4. Find the motorbike constant speed

    The car acceleration is $\dfrac{V}{30}$. The motorbike accelerates for $40$ s, so its speed is $40\cdot\dfrac{V}{30}=\dfrac{4V}{3}$.

  5. Find the motorbike distance

    Its distance is $\dfrac12(40)\left(\dfrac{4V}{3}\right)+10\left(\dfrac{4V}{3}\right)+\dfrac12\left(\dfrac{4V}{3}+V\right)(T-60)$.

  6. Simplify the motorbike distance

    This becomes $\dfrac{80V}{3}+\dfrac{40V}{3}+\dfrac{7V}{6}(T-60)=40V+\dfrac{7V}{6}(T-60)=\dfrac{7VT}{6}-30V$.

  7. Equate the two distances

    At time $T$, the distances are equal: $V(T-15)=\dfrac{7VT}{6}-30V$. Divide by $V$ to get $T-15=\dfrac{7T}{6}-30$.

  8. Find T

    Multiplying by $6$ gives $6T-90=7T-180$, so $T=90$.

Final answer

$(a)\ \text{car distance}=V(T-15)$. $(b)$ Motorbike graph starts at $t=10$, reaches $\dfrac{4V}{3}$ at $t=50$, stays constant to $t=60$, then falls to $V$ at $t=T$. $(c)\ \text{constant speed}=\dfrac{4V}{3}.\quad (d)\ T=90$.

  1. Describe the speed-time graph

    The graph starts at $(0,0)$, rises in a straight line with gradient $0.8$ until time $T$ and speed $V$, then stays horizontal at speed $V$ until $t=30$.

  2. Write V in terms of T

    A more realistic model could include reaction time at the start, and could replace the instantaneous change from acceleration to constant speed with a smoother transition.

  1. Resolve B's velocity

    A bearing of $150^\circ$ is $30^\circ$ east of south. Therefore the east component is $40\sin150^\circ=20$ and the north component is $40\cos150^\circ=-20\sqrt3$.

  2. Write B's velocity

    $\mathbf v_B=20\mathbf i-20\sqrt3\mathbf j\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$.

  3. Write A's position

    Boat A starts at $60\mathbf i$ and travels due west at $20\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$, so $\mathbf r=60\mathbf i-20t\mathbf i=(60-20t)\mathbf i$.

  4. Write B's position

    Boat B starts at O, so $\mathbf s=t(20\mathbf i-20\sqrt3\mathbf j)=20t\mathbf i-20\sqrt3t\mathbf j$.

  5. Find the separation vector

    $\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf s-\mathbf r=(20t-(60-20t))\mathbf i-20\sqrt3t\mathbf j=(40t-60)\mathbf i-20\sqrt3t\mathbf j$.

  6. Use the noon distance

    At noon the distance between the boats is $60$ km. Set the later distance equal to $60$: $(40t-60)^2+(-20\sqrt3t)^2=60^2$.

  7. Solve for t

    Expanding gives $2800t^2-4800t=0$, so $t(2800t-4800)=0$. The later time is $t=\dfrac{4800}{2800}=\dfrac{12}{7}$ hours.

  8. Convert to clock time

    $\dfrac{12}{7}$ hours is $1$ hour and $42.857\ldots$ minutes after noon, which rounds to $1{:}43$ pm, or $13{:}43$.

Final answer

$(a)\ \mathbf v_B=20\mathbf i-20\sqrt3\mathbf j\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}.\quad (b)\ \mathbf r=(60-20t)\mathbf i,\ \mathbf s=20t\mathbf i-20\sqrt3t\mathbf j.\quad (c)\ 13{:}43$, or $1{:}43\ \mathrm{pm}$.
