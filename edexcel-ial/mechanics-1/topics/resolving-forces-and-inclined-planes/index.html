1. WME01/01 January 2026 · Q1

Medium6 marks · main topic: Momentum, Impulse & Collisions
Show worked solution
  1. Choose a sign convention

    Take the initial direction of P as positive. Then before impact, $v_P=3u$ and $v_Q=-4u$. The impulse on P acts opposite to P\'s initial motion, so its signed impulse is $-\dfrac{33}{2}mu$.

  2. Use impulse on P

    For P, impulse equals change in momentum: $-\dfrac{33}{2}mu=3m(v_P-3u)$. Dividing by $3m$ gives $v_P-3u=-\dfrac{11u}{2}$, so $v_P=-\dfrac{5u}{2}$.

  3. Interpret the speed of P

    The negative sign means P has reversed direction. Its speed is therefore $\left|-\dfrac{5u}{2}\right|=\dfrac{5u}{2}$.

  4. Use the equal and opposite impulse on Q

    Q receives impulse $+\dfrac{33}{2}mu$ in the positive direction. Hence $\dfrac{33}{2}mu=5m(v_Q-(-4u))=5m(v_Q+4u)$.

  5. Interpret the speed of Q

    Dividing by $5m$ gives $v_Q+4u=\dfrac{33u}{10}$, so $v_Q=-\dfrac{7u}{10}$. Q is still moving in its original direction, and its speed is $\dfrac{7u}{10}$.

Final answer

$(i)\ \text{speed of P}=\dfrac{5u}{2}.\quad (ii)\ \text{speed of Q}=\dfrac{7u}{10}$.

  1. Part (a): Find w in terms of u - Apply impulse-momentum theorem to Q

    $\text{Impulse} = \text{Final momentum} - \text{Initial momentum}$ For particle Q (mass = 3m): ; Initial velocity: $-u$ (moving left, negative) ; Final velocity: $+2w$ (moving right, positive) $\text{Impulse} = 3m(2w) - 3m(-u)$ $\frac{7mu}{2} = 6mw + 3mu$ $\frac{7mu}{2} = 3m(2w + u)$ $\frac{7u}{2} = 6w + 3u$ $6w = \frac{7u}{2} - 3u$ $6w = \frac{7u - 6u}{2}$ $6w = \frac{u}{2}$ $w = \frac{u}{12}$

  2. Part (b): Find the two possible values of k - Apply conservation of momentum

    $\text{Total momentum before} = \text{Total momentum after}$ Before collision: $\text{P momentum} = 7m \times ku = 7mku \text{ (right)}$ $\text{Q momentum} = 3m \times (-u) = -3mu \text{ (left)}$ $\text{Total} = 7mku - 3mu$ After collision: $\text{P momentum} = 7m \times w = 7mw \text{ (right)}$ $\text{Q momentum} = 3m \times 2w = 6mw \text{ (right)}$ $\text{Total} = 7mw + 6mw = 13mw$

  3. Part (b): Find the two possible values of k - Set up equation

    $7mku - 3mu = 13mw$ $7ku - 3u = 13w$

  4. Part (b): Find the two possible values of k - Substitute $w = \frac{u}{12}$

    $7ku - 3u = 13 \times \frac{u}{12}$ $7ku - 3u = \frac{13u}{12}$ $7ku = \frac{13u}{12} + 3u$ $7ku = \frac{13u + 36u}{12}$ $7ku = \frac{49u}{12}$ $7k = \frac{49}{12}$ $k = \frac{49}{84} = \frac{7}{12}$

  5. Part (b): Find the two possible values of k - Consider both directions for P after collision

    P could move either right (velocity = $+w$) or left (velocity = $-w$) Case 1: P moves right with velocity $+w$ Already solved: $k = \frac{7}{12}$ Case 2: P moves left with velocity $-w$ Total momentum after = $-7mw + 6mw = -mw$ $7mku - 3mu = -mw$ $7ku - 3u = -w$ $7ku - 3u = -\frac{u}{12}$ $7ku = 3u - \frac{u}{12}$ $7ku = \frac{36u - u}{12}$ $7ku = \frac{35u}{12}$ $k = \frac{35}{84} = \frac{5}{12}$

Final answer

$(a)\ w=\dfrac{u}{12}.\quad (b)\ k=\dfrac{7}{12}\ \text{or}\ k=\dfrac{5}{12}$.

  1. Use the trig ratios

    Since $\tan\alpha=\dfrac34$, $\sin\alpha=\dfrac35$ and $\cos\alpha=\dfrac45$.

  2. Resolve perpendicular to the plane

    The force $X$ acts along the plane, so the normal reaction is $R=mg\cos\alpha=\dfrac45mg$.

  3. Use friction

    The limiting friction is $F=\mu R=\dfrac45\mu mg$.

  4. Use the sliding-up condition

    When $X=2P$, the particle is about to slide up, so friction acts down the plane. Hence $2P=mg\sin\alpha+F=\dfrac35mg+\dfrac45\mu mg$.

  5. Use the sliding-down condition

    When $X=P$, the particle is about to slide down, so friction acts up the plane. Hence $P+F=mg\sin\alpha$, so $P=\dfrac35mg-\dfrac45\mu mg$.

  6. Eliminate P

    Double the second equation: $2P=\dfrac65mg-\dfrac85\mu mg$. This equals the first expression for $2P$.

  7. Solve for mu

    $\dfrac65mg-\dfrac85\mu mg=\dfrac35mg+\dfrac45\mu mg$. Cancel $mg$ and multiply by $5$: $6-8\mu=3+4\mu$. Hence $3=12\mu$ and $\mu=\dfrac14$.

Final answer

$\mu=\dfrac14$.

  1. Set the support positions

    The beam length is $8a$. C is at distance $a$ from A, and D is at distance $6a$ from A because $DB=2a$.

  2. Name the reactions

    Let the reaction at C be $R$. The reaction at D is $3R$.

  3. Use vertical equilibrium

    $R+3R=60g$, so $4R=60g$ and $R=15g$.

    The beam's weight acts at distance $x$ from A. Hence $60g x=R(a)+3R(6a)$.

  5. Find x

    Substitute $R=15g$: $60g x=15ga+45g(6a)=285ga$. Thus $x=\dfrac{285a}{60}=\dfrac{19a}{4}$.

  6. Use equal reactions with the box

    When the box is added, let each support reaction be $S$. Vertical equilibrium gives $2S=(60+M)g$.

    Substitute $S=\dfrac{(60+M)g}{2}$ and cancel $ag$: $285+2M=\dfrac{7}{2}(60+M)=210+\dfrac72M$. Hence $75=\dfrac32M$ and $M=50$.

  1. Use impulse to find lambda

    P starts from rest and immediately after the impulse has speed $5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Since the mass of P is $3m$, $\lambda=3m(5-0)=15m$.

    Between the impulse and the collision, P slows from $5$ to $2.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ over $1.4$ m. Using $v^2=u^2+2as$: $(2.5)^2=5^2+2a(1.4)$.

  4. Connect friction to acceleration

  5. Find mu

    $\mu=\dfrac{-a}{g}=\dfrac{6.696\ldots}{9.8}=0.683\ldots$, so $\mu=0.68$ to 2 significant figures.

  1. Resolve parallel to the plane

    P is on the point of moving up the plane, so friction acts down the plane. The tension has component $0.75mg\cos\alpha$ up the plane, and the weight component is $mg\sin\alpha$ down the plane.

  2. Find the frictional force

    For equilibrium parallel to the plane, $0.75mg\cos\alpha=mg\sin\alpha+F$. Hence $F=0.75mg\cos\alpha-mg\sin\alpha$.

  3. Resolve perpendicular to the plane

    The string has a component away from the plane, so $R=mg\cos\alpha-0.75mg\sin\alpha$.

  4. Use the coefficient of friction

    Given $\mu=\dfrac12$, limiting friction gives $F=\dfrac12R$. Therefore $0.75mg\cos\alpha-mg\sin\alpha=\dfrac12(mg\cos\alpha-0.75mg\sin\alpha)$.

  5. Show the tangent result

    Divide by $mg$ and collect terms: $0.75\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha=0.5\cos\alpha-0.375\sin\alpha$. Thus $0.25\cos\alpha=0.625\sin\alpha$, so $\tan\alpha=\dfrac{0.25}{0.625}=\dfrac25$.

  6. Check what happens when the string breaks

    Without the string, $R=mg\cos\alpha$ and the maximum friction is $\dfrac12mg\cos\alpha$. The component of weight down the plane is $mg\sin\alpha$.

  7. Compare friction with the downslope component

    Since $\tan\alpha=\dfrac25<\dfrac12$, we have $mg\sin\alpha<\dfrac12mg\cos\alpha$. The available friction is large enough to hold P, so P remains at rest.

Final answer

$(a)\ F=0.75mg\cos\alpha-mg\sin\alpha.\quad (b)\ \tan\alpha=\dfrac25.\quad (c)\ P\ \text{remains at rest}$.

    At C, with the pulling force removed, $R=2g\cos30^\circ$. The maximum friction is $0.3R=0.3(2g\cos30^\circ)=5.09\ldots\ \mathrm{N}$.

  9. Compare with the downslope weight component

    The component of weight down the plane is $2g\sin30^\circ=9.8\ \mathrm{N}$. Since $9.8>5.09\ldots$, friction is not large enough to hold P at rest, so P will not remain at rest at C.

Final answer

$(a)\ a=1.18\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\ \text{up the plane}.\quad (b)\ v=3.98\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\approx4.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.\quad (c)$ P will not remain at rest at C.

  1. Use the trig ratios

    Since $\tan\alpha=\dfrac34$, $\sin\alpha=\dfrac35$ and $\cos\alpha=\dfrac45$.

  2. Find friction on A

    For A, $R=2g\cos\alpha=\dfrac{8g}{5}$. With coefficient $\dfrac14$, friction is $F=\dfrac14R=\dfrac{2g}{5}$.

  3. Equation for A

    A moves up the plane. Taking up the plane as positive: $T-2g\sin\alpha-F=2a$.

  4. Simplify A's equation

    $T-2g\cdot\dfrac35-\dfrac{2g}{5}=2a$, so $T-\dfrac{8g}{5}=2a$.

  5. Equation for B

    B descends. Taking downward as positive: $4g-T=4a$.

  6. Solve for T

    From A, $T=2a+\dfrac{8g}{5}$. From B, $T=4g-4a$. Equating gives $6a=\dfrac{12g}{5}$, so $a=\dfrac{2g}{5}$. Then $T=4g-4\left(\dfrac{2g}{5}\right)=\dfrac{12g}{5}$ N.

  7. Find speed when B reaches the ground

    Before B hits the ground, both particles move distance $h$ from rest with acceleration $\dfrac{2g}{5}$. Thus $v^2=2\left(\dfrac{2g}{5}\right)h=\dfrac{4gh}{5}$.

  8. Find extra distance after B stops

    After B hits the ground, A continues up the plane. The forces down the plane on A are $2g\sin\alpha+F=\dfrac{6g}{5}+\dfrac{2g}{5}=\dfrac{8g}{5}$, so its deceleration is $\dfrac{4g}{5}$.

  9. Calculate extra distance

    Let the extra distance be $s$. Then $0=\dfrac{4gh}{5}-2\left(\dfrac{4g}{5}\right)s$, so $s=\dfrac{h}{2}$.

  10. Find the range for d

    A travels total distance $h+\dfrac{h}{2}=\dfrac{3h}{2}$ before stopping. Since it comes to rest before reaching the pulley, $d>\dfrac{3h}{2}$.

  11. Name a more realistic factor

    A more realistic model could include friction at the pulley.

Final answer

$(a)\ T=\dfrac{12g}{5}\ \mathrm{N}=23.5\ \mathrm{N}.\quad (b)\ d>\dfrac{3h}{2}.\quad (c)$ A more realistic model could include friction at the pulley.
