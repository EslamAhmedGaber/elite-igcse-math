1. WME01/01 October 2023 · Q1

Easy5 marks · main topic: Moments
Show worked solution
  1. Name the tensions

    Let the tension at D be $T$. The tension at C is double this, so it is $2T$.

  2. Use vertical equilibrium

    The beam is in equilibrium, so the upward forces equal the weight: $2T+T=24$. Hence $3T=24$ and $T=8\ \mathrm{N}$.

  3. Set the moment distances

    The beam is uniform, so its weight acts at the midpoint, $3$ m from A. Also $AC=x$ and $AD=x+2$.

  4. Take moments about A

    Clockwise and anticlockwise moments balance: $(2T)x+T(x+2)=24\cdot3$.

  5. Solve for x

    Substitute $T=8$: $16x+8(x+2)=72$. Thus $24x+16=72$, so $24x=56$ and $x=\dfrac{7}{3}$ m.

Final answer

$(i)\ T_D=8\ \mathrm{N}.\quad (ii)\ x=\dfrac{7}{3}\ \mathrm{m}\approx2.33\ \mathrm{m}$.

3. WME01/01 January 2026 · Q1

Medium6 marks · main topic: Momentum, Impulse & Collisions
Show worked solution
  1. Choose a sign convention

    Take the initial direction of P as positive. Then before impact, $v_P=3u$ and $v_Q=-4u$. The impulse on P acts opposite to P\'s initial motion, so its signed impulse is $-\dfrac{33}{2}mu$.

  2. Use impulse on P

    For P, impulse equals change in momentum: $-\dfrac{33}{2}mu=3m(v_P-3u)$. Dividing by $3m$ gives $v_P-3u=-\dfrac{11u}{2}$, so $v_P=-\dfrac{5u}{2}$.

  3. Interpret the speed of P

    The negative sign means P has reversed direction. Its speed is therefore $\left|-\dfrac{5u}{2}\right|=\dfrac{5u}{2}$.

  4. Use the equal and opposite impulse on Q

    Q receives impulse $+\dfrac{33}{2}mu$ in the positive direction. Hence $\dfrac{33}{2}mu=5m(v_Q-(-4u))=5m(v_Q+4u)$.

  5. Interpret the speed of Q

    Dividing by $5m$ gives $v_Q+4u=\dfrac{33u}{10}$, so $v_Q=-\dfrac{7u}{10}$. Q is still moving in its original direction, and its speed is $\dfrac{7u}{10}$.

Final answer

$(i)\ \text{speed of P}=\dfrac{5u}{2}.\quad (ii)\ \text{speed of Q}=\dfrac{7u}{10}$.

4. WME01/01 May/June 2025 · Q1

Medium6 marks · main topic: Momentum, Impulse & Collisions

  1. Part (a): Find v in terms of u - Apply impulse-momentum theorem to Q

    $\text{Impulse} = \text{Final momentum} - \text{Initial momentum}$ For particle Q: ; Mass = $3m$ ; Initial velocity = $2u$ ; Final velocity = $v$ ; Impulse received = $4.5mu$

  2. Part (a): Find v in terms of u - Set up equation

    $4.5mu = 3m \times v - 3m \times 2u$ $4.5mu = 3mv - 6mu$ $4.5u = 3v - 6u$ $4.5u + 6u = 3v$ $10.5u = 3v$ $v = \frac{10.5u}{3}$ $v = 3.5u$

  3. Part (b): Find the value of k - Apply conservation of momentum

    $\text{Total momentum before} = \text{Total momentum after}$ Before collision: $\text{P momentum} = km \times 5u = 5kmu$ $\text{Q momentum} = 3m \times 2u = 6mu$ $\text{Total} = 5kmu + 6mu$ After collision: $\text{P momentum} = km \times 2u = 2kmu$ $\text{Q momentum} = 3m \times 3.5u = 10.5mu$ $\text{Total} = 2kmu + 10.5mu$

  4. Part (b): Find the value of k - Set up equation

    $5kmu + 6mu = 2kmu + 10.5mu$ $5km + 6m = 2km + 10.5m$ $5k + 6 = 2k + 10.5$ $5k - 2k = 10.5 - 6$ $3k = 4.5$ $k = \frac{4.5}{3}$ $k = 1.5$

Final answer

$(a)\ v=3.5u=\dfrac{7u}{2}.\quad (b)\ k=1.5=\dfrac{3}{2}$.

  1. Choose a sign convention

    Take the initial direction of A as positive. Before collision, A has velocity $2x$ and B has velocity $-x$. After collision, both reverse direction, so A has velocity $-y$ and B has velocity $5y$.

  2. Apply conservation of momentum

    $4m(2x)+3m(-x)=4m(-y)+3m(5y)$. The left side is $5mx$ and the right side is $11my$.

  3. Show the ratio

    $5mx=11my$, so $\dfrac{y}{x}=\dfrac{5}{11}$.

  4. Use change in momentum for A

    The impulse received by A is the change in momentum of A: $I=4m|-y-2x|=4m(y+2x)$.

  5. Substitute y

    Using $y=\dfrac{5x}{11}$, $I=4m\left(\dfrac{5x}{11}+2x\right)=4m\left(\dfrac{27x}{11}\right)=\dfrac{108}{11}mx$.

Final answer

$(a)\ \dfrac{y}{x}=\dfrac{5}{11}.\quad (b)\ I=\dfrac{108}{11}mx$.

  1. Use the lower particle to find acceleration

    For P, take downward as positive. Its weight is $4mg$ and the tension in the string between P and Q is $3mg$ upward, so $4mg-3mg=4ma$.

  2. Find the acceleration

    The equation gives $mg=4ma$, hence $a=\dfrac{g}{4}$ downward.

  3. Write the equation for Q

    For Q, downward forces are its weight $2mg$ and the lower string tension $3mg$. The upper string tension $T$ acts upward. Taking downward as positive: $2mg+3mg-T=2ma$.

    $5mg-T=2m\left(\dfrac{g}{4}\right)=\dfrac{mg}{2}$.

  5. Find the upper tension

  1. Choose a positive direction

    Take P's original direction as positive. Before collision, P has velocity $3u$ and Q has velocity $-2u$.

  2. Use impulse on P

    The impulse on P is opposite to P's original motion and has magnitude $5mu$. Let P's velocity after collision be $v$. Then $-5mu=2m(v-3u)$.

18. WME01/01 January 2023 · Q7

Medium7 marks · main topic: Newton's Second Law
Show worked solution
  1. Define the box mass

    Let the combined mass of boxes P and Q be $M$. The lift itself has mass $m$, so the total moving mass is $m+M$.

  2. Apply Newtons second law to the whole system

    Taking upward as positive, $\dfrac{42}{5}mg-(m+M)g=(m+M)\dfrac{2g}{5}$.

  3. Find M

    Move the weight term to the right: $\dfrac{42}{5}mg=(m+M)\left(g+\dfrac{2g}{5}\right)=\dfrac{7g}{5}(m+M)$. Hence $42m=7(m+M)$ and $M=5m$.

  4. Let the mass of P be p

    For box P alone, the upward force from Q is $\dfrac{14}{5}mg$, while its weight is $pg$. The acceleration is still $\dfrac{2g}{5}$ upward.

  5. Apply Newtons second law to P

    $\dfrac{14}{5}mg-pg=p\dfrac{2g}{5}$.

  6. Find p

    Divide by $g$: $\dfrac{14}{5}m=p+\dfrac{2p}{5}=\dfrac{7p}{5}$. Therefore $p=2m$.

Final answer

$(a)\ \text{combined mass of P and Q}=5m.\quad (b)\ \text{mass of P}=2m$.

  1. Choose a positive direction

    Take P's original direction as positive. Q initially has velocity $-2u$. Since Q reverses direction and then has speed $3u$, its final velocity is $3u$.

  2. Find the impulse on Q

    $0.4T^2+24T-0.8T^2=200$, so $-0.4T^2+24T-200=0$. Multiplying by $-2.5$ gives $T^2-60T+500=0$, so $k=60$.

  6. Solve and choose the valid root

    $T^2-60T+500=(T-10)(T-50)=0$, so $T=10$ or $50$. Since the whole race lasts $30$ s, $T=10$.

  7. Give two model improvements

    A more realistic model could include reaction time at the start, and could replace the instantaneous change from acceleration to constant speed with a smoother transition.

Final answer

$(a)$ Speed-time graph rises linearly to $V$ at $t=T$, then is horizontal until $t=30$. $(b)\ V=0.8T.\quad (c)\ T^2-60T+500=0$, so $k=60$. $(d)\ T=10$. $(e)$ For example, include reaction time at the start and avoid the instantaneous change to constant speed.
