1. WME01/01 May/June 2022 · Q1

Easy4 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Use impulse as change in momentum

    Take the original direction of Q as positive. Q has mass $3m$ and initial velocity $2u$. Let its velocity after collision be $v$.

  2. Set up the impulse equation

    The impulse has magnitude $4mu$ and acts opposite to Q's original motion, so $4mu=3m(2u-v)$.

  3. Find the speed

    Cancel $m$: $4u=6u-3v$. Thus $3v=2u$ and $v=\dfrac{2u}{3}$.

  4. State the direction

    Since $v$ is positive in Q's original direction, Q continues in the same direction after the collision.

Final answer

$(a)\ \text{speed}=\dfrac{2u}{3}.\quad (b)\ \text{same direction as before the collision}$.

2. WME01/01 May/June 2024 · Q1

Easy5 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Use conservation of momentum

    When the string becomes taut, A and B move with the same speed $S$. Before this, only A is moving. Hence $mU=(m+3m)S=4mS$.

  2. Find the common speed

    From $mU=4mS$, cancel $m$ to get $S=\dfrac{U}{4}$.

  3. Use impulse on A

    The impulse on A equals the magnitude of its change in momentum: $I=m\left|\dfrac{U}{4}-U\right|$.

  4. Simplify the impulse

    $I=m\left(\dfrac{3U}{4}\right)=\dfrac{3mU}{4}$.

Final answer

$(a)\ S=\dfrac{U}{4}.\quad (b)\ I=\dfrac{3mU}{4}$.

4. WME01/01 January 2026 · Q1

Easy6 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Choose a sign convention

    Take the initial direction of P as positive. Then before impact, $v_P=3u$ and $v_Q=-4u$. The impulse on P acts opposite to P\'s initial motion, so its signed impulse is $-\dfrac{33}{2}mu$.

  2. Use impulse on P

    For P, impulse equals change in momentum: $-\dfrac{33}{2}mu=3m(v_P-3u)$. Dividing by $3m$ gives $v_P-3u=-\dfrac{11u}{2}$, so $v_P=-\dfrac{5u}{2}$.

  3. Interpret the speed of P

    The negative sign means P has reversed direction. Its speed is therefore $\left|-\dfrac{5u}{2}\right|=\dfrac{5u}{2}$.

  4. Use the equal and opposite impulse on Q

    Q receives impulse $+\dfrac{33}{2}mu$ in the positive direction. Hence $\dfrac{33}{2}mu=5m(v_Q-(-4u))=5m(v_Q+4u)$.

  5. Interpret the speed of Q

    Dividing by $5m$ gives $v_Q+4u=\dfrac{33u}{10}$, so $v_Q=-\dfrac{7u}{10}$. Q is still moving in its original direction, and its speed is $\dfrac{7u}{10}$.

Final answer

$(i)\ \text{speed of P}=\dfrac{5u}{2}.\quad (ii)\ \text{speed of Q}=\dfrac{7u}{10}$.

  1. Choose a sign convention

    Take the initial direction of A as positive. Before collision, A has velocity $2x$ and B has velocity $-x$. After collision, both reverse direction, so A has velocity $-y$ and B has velocity $5y$.

  2. Apply conservation of momentum

    $4m(2x)+3m(-x)=4m(-y)+3m(5y)$. The left side is $5mx$ and the right side is $11my$.

  3. Show the ratio

    $5mx=11my$, so $\dfrac{y}{x}=\dfrac{5}{11}$.

  4. Use change in momentum for A

    The impulse received by A is the change in momentum of A: $I=4m|-y-2x|=4m(y+2x)$.

  5. Substitute y

    Using $y=\dfrac{5x}{11}$, $I=4m\left(\dfrac{5x}{11}+2x\right)=4m\left(\dfrac{27x}{11}\right)=\dfrac{108}{11}mx$.

Final answer

$(a)\ \dfrac{y}{x}=\dfrac{5}{11}.\quad (b)\ I=\dfrac{108}{11}mx$.

  1. Choose a positive direction

    Take P's original direction as positive. Before collision, $v_P=u$ and $v_Q=-u$. After collision, Q has velocity $\dfrac{u}{3}$ because its direction is reversed.

  2. Find the impulse on Q

    Impulse on Q equals change in momentum: $I=m_2\left(\dfrac{u}{3}-(-u)\right)=m_2\left(\dfrac{4u}{3}\right)=\dfrac{4}{3}m_2u$.

  3. Use conservation of momentum

    Let the speed of P after collision be $v$. Since P reverses direction, its velocity is $-v$. Hence $m_1u-m_2u=-m_1v+\dfrac13m_2u$.

  4. Solve for P's speed

    Rearrange: $m_1v=\dfrac43m_2u-m_1u$, so $v=\dfrac{(4m_2-3m_1)u}{3m_1}$.

  5. Use the fact that speed is positive

    Since $u>0$ and $m_1>0$, the numerator must be positive: $4m_2-3m_1>0$. Therefore $m_2>\dfrac34m_1$.

Final answer

$(a)\ I=\dfrac{4}{3}m_2u.\quad (b)\ \text{speed of P}=\dfrac{(4m_2-3m_1)u}{3m_1}.\quad (c)\ m_2>\dfrac34m_1$.

  1. Find the time to the first hit

    The ball is released from rest and falls $40$ m. Use $s=ut+\dfrac12gt^2$: $40=\dfrac12gt_1^2$. Thus $t_1=\sqrt{\dfrac{80}{g}}=\dfrac{20}{7}$ s.

  2. Find the speed just before the first bounce

    Using $v^2=u^2+2gs$, $v_1^2=2g(40)$, so $v_1=28\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

  3. Use the rebound speed

    After the first bounce the speed is reduced by $50\%$, so the upward speed is $14\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

  4. Find the time until the second hit

    The time to go up and return to the ground is $\dfrac{2(14)}{g}=\dfrac{20}{7}$ s.

  5. Find the total time

    Total time to the second hit is $\dfrac{20}{7}+\dfrac{20}{7}=\dfrac{40}{7}=5.71$ s approximately.

  6. Find the speed after the third bounce

    After each bounce, the speed is halved. The upward speeds after the first, second and third bounces are $14$, $7$ and $3.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

  7. Find the height after the third bounce

    At the top after the third bounce, $0=(3.5)^2-2gh$. Therefore $h=\dfrac{(3.5)^2}{2g}=0.625$ m.

Final answer

$(a)\ 5.71\ \mathrm{s}\text{ approximately}.\quad (b)\ 0.625\ \mathrm{m}$.

  1. Use conservation of momentum

    Take P's original direction as positive. Before collision, P has velocity $3u$ and Q is at rest. After collision, Q has velocity $2u$. Let P's velocity be $v$.

  2. Find P velocity

    $2m(3u)=2mv+4m(2u)$. Thus $6mu=2mv+8mu$, so $2mv=-2mu$ and $v=-u$.

  3. State speed and direction

    The speed of P is $u$. The negative sign shows that P moves in the opposite direction after the collision.

  4. Find the deceleration of Q

    After collision, Q starts with speed $2u$ and stops after distance $\dfrac{6u^2}{g}$. Use $v^2=u^2+2as$: $0=(2u)^2+2a\left(\dfrac{6u^2}{g}\right)$.

  5. Simplify acceleration

    $0=4u^2+\dfrac{12au^2}{g}$, so $a=-\dfrac{g}{3}$. The deceleration magnitude is $\dfrac{g}{3}$.

  6. Use friction on Q

    For Q, $R=4mg$, so friction is $\mu R=4\mu mg$. This friction produces deceleration: $4\mu mg=4m\cdot\dfrac{g}{3}$.

  7. Find mu

    Cancel $4mg$: $\mu=\dfrac13$.

Final answer

$(a)\ \text{speed of P}=u.\quad (b)\ \text{P reverses direction}.\quad (c)\ \mu=\dfrac13$.

  1. Use impulse to find lambda

    P starts from rest and immediately after the impulse has speed $5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Since the mass of P is $3m$, $\lambda=3m(5-0)=15m$.

  2. Find the acceleration before collision

    Between the impulse and the collision, P slows from $5$ to $2.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ over $1.4$ m. Using $v^2=u^2+2as$: $(2.5)^2=5^2+2a(1.4)$.

  4. Connect friction to acceleration

    On the horizontal surface, $R=3mg$ and friction is $F=\mu R=3\mu mg$. This friction is opposite the motion, so $-3\mu mg=3ma$ and therefore $a=-\mu g$.

  6. Use momentum in the collision

    Immediately before collision, P has speed $2.5$ and Q is at rest. Immediately after, Q has speed $2.1$ and P has speed $v$. Conservation of momentum gives $3m(2.5)+m(0)=3mv+m(2.1)$.

    The length of time above C is $4.438\ldots-0.459\ldots=3.98\ldots\ \mathrm{s}$, about $4.0\ \mathrm{s}$.

    Particle P (mass $kM$) moves upward with acceleration $\frac{g}{5}$: ; Tension T (upward) ; Weight $kMg$ (downward)

  2. Part (a): Write equation of motion for P - Apply Newton's Second Law

    Taking upward as positive: $\sum F = ma$ $T - kMg = kM \times \frac{g}{5}$

  3. Part (b): Find value of k - Write equation of motion for Q

    Particle Q (mass $3M$) moves downward with acceleration $\frac{g}{5}$: Taking downward as positive for Q: $3Mg - T = 3M \times \frac{g}{5}$ $3Mg - T = \frac{3Mg}{5}$ $T = 3Mg - \frac{3Mg}{5}$ $T = \frac{12Mg}{5}$

  4. Part (b): Find value of k - Substitute into equation for P

    From part (a): $T - kMg = \frac{kMg}{5}$ $\frac{12Mg}{5} - kMg = \frac{kMg}{5}$ $\frac{12Mg}{5} = kMg + \frac{kMg}{5}$ $\frac{12Mg}{5} = \frac{5kMg + kMg}{5}$ $\frac{12Mg}{5} = \frac{6kMg}{5}$ $12 = 6k$ $k = 2$

  5. Part (c): Find force on pulley while Q moving down - Identify tension in string

    From part (b): $T = \frac{12Mg}{5}$ Given: $M = 0.5$ kg $T = \frac{12 \times 0.5 \times g}{5}$ $= \frac{6g}{5}$ $= \frac{6 \times 9.8}{5}$ $= 11.76 \text{ N}$

  6. Part (c): Find force on pulley while Q moving down - Find force on pulley

    The pulley experiences two tension forces: ; Horizontal tension from P: $T = 11.76$ N ; Vertical tension from Q: $T = 11.76$ N These are perpendicular, so use Pythagoras: $F = \sqrt{T^2 + T^2}$ $= \sqrt{2T^2}$ $= T\sqrt{2}$ $= 11.76\sqrt{2}$ $= 16.63...$ $= 16.6 \text{ N (3 sf)}$ Or: $F = \frac{6g\sqrt{2}}{5} = \frac{12g}{5} = 2.4g = 23.5$ N Wait, let me recalculate...   7. Part (d): Find impulse when Q hits ground - Find velocity of Q just before hitting ground

    Using SUVAT with: ; $u = 0$ (starts from rest) ; $a = \frac{g}{5} = \frac{9.8}{5} = 1.96$ m s$^{-2}$ ; $s = 2.5$ m $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ $v^2 = 0 + 2 \times \frac{g}{5} \times 2.5$ $v^2 = \frac{5g}{5}$ $v^2 = g$ $v = \sqrt{g} = \sqrt{9.8} = 3.130... \text{ m s}^{-1}$ Or exact: $v = \sqrt{g} = \sqrt{\frac{7g}{5}}$ Actually: $v^2 = 2 \times 1.96 \times 2.5 = 9.8$, so $v = \sqrt{9.8} = 3.13$ m s$^{-1}$

  8. Part (d): Find impulse when Q hits ground - Calculate impulse

    $\text{Impulse} = m(v_{\text{after}} - v_{\text{before}})$ Taking downward as positive before impact: ; Velocity before: $v_{\text{before}} = +3.13$ m s$^{-1}$ (downward) ; Velocity after: $v_{\text{after}} = -0.4$ m s$^{-1}$ (upward, rebounds) ; Mass of Q: $3M = 3 \times 0.5 = 1.5$ kg $I = 1.5 \times (-0.4 - 3.13)$ $= 1.5 \times (-3.53)$ $= -5.295...$ $|I| = 5.3 \text{ N s (2 sf)}$ Or using exact values: $|I| = 1.5(\sqrt{g} + 0.4) = 1.5(3.13 + 0.4) = 5.3$ N s

  9. Part (e): Find total time until P first comes to rest - Time for Q to fall ($t_1$)

    Using $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ with $u = 0$, $s = 2.5$ m, $a = \frac{g}{5}$: $2.5 = 0 + \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{g}{5} \times t_1^2$ $2.5 = \frac{g t_1^2}{10}$ $25 = g t_1^2$ $t_1^2 = \frac{25}{9.8} = 2.551...$ $t_1 = \sqrt{\frac{25}{9.8}} = 1.597... \text{ s}$ Or exact: $t_1 = \sqrt{\frac{5 \times 5}{g}} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{g}}$ Better: $t_1 = \sqrt{\frac{5}{g/5}} = \sqrt{\frac{25}{g}}$ Actually: $2.5 = \frac{1}{2} \times 1.96 \times t_1^2$ $t_1^2 = \frac{5}{1.96} = 2.551$, so $t_1 = 1.597$ s

  10. Part (e): Find total time until P first comes to rest - Velocity of P when Q hits ground

  11. Part (e): Find total time until P first comes to rest - Time for P to come to rest ($t_2$)

    After Q hits ground, string goes slack. P continues upward under gravity alone. Using $v = u + at$ with $v = 0$, $u = 3.13$ m s$^{-1}$, $a = -g$: $0 = 3.13 - 9.8 t_2$ $t_2 = \frac{3.13}{9.8}$ $= 0.3194... \text{ s}$ Or: $t_2 = \frac{\sqrt{g}}{g} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}$
