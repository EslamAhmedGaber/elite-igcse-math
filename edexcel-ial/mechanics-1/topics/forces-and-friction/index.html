1. WME01/01 May/June 2024 · Q2

Easy4 marks

  1. Resolve the two forces

    Take east as $\mathbf{i}$ and north as $\mathbf{j}$. The force due west is $-10\mathbf{i}$. A bearing of $330^\circ$ is $30^\circ$ west of north, so the 8 N force has components $-8\sin30^\circ\mathbf{i}+8\cos30^\circ\mathbf{j}$.

  2. Add the components

    $\mathbf{F}=(-10-4)\mathbf{i}+4\sqrt3\mathbf{j}=-14\mathbf{i}+4\sqrt3\mathbf{j}$.

  3. Find the magnitude

    $|\mathbf{F}|=\sqrt{(-14)^2+(4\sqrt3)^2}=\sqrt{196+48}=\sqrt{244}=2\sqrt{61}\ \mathrm{N}$.

  4. Give a decimal value

    $2\sqrt{61}=15.620\ldots$, so the magnitude is about $16\ \mathrm{N}$ to 2 significant figures.

Final answer

$|\mathbf{F}|=2\sqrt{61}\ \mathrm{N}\approx15.6\ \mathrm{N}$.

2. WME01/01 May/June 2025 · Q5

Easy6 marks · main topic: Moments
Show worked solution
  1. Setup Diagrams - Scenario 1 - Tilting about A

    Taking moments about A: $\sum M_A = 0$ Clockwise moments = Anticlockwise moments $P \times 3a = W \times d$ $3aP = Wd \quad \text{...(1)}$

  2. Setup Diagrams - Scenario 2 - Tilting about C

    Taking moments about C: $\sum M_C = 0$ Distance from C to COM = $(2a - d)$ Distance from C to B = $a$ $1.25P \times a = W \times (2a - d)$ $1.25Pa = W(2a - d)$ $1.25Pa = 2aW - Wd \quad \text{...(2)}$

  3. Setup Diagrams - Substitute equation (1) into equation (2)

    From (1): $Wd = 3aP$ $1.25Pa = 2aW - 3aP$ $1.25Pa + 3aP = 2aW$ $4.25Pa = 2aW$ $P = \frac{2aW}{4.25a}$ $P = \frac{2W}{4.25}$ Substitute back into (1): $3a \times \frac{2W}{4.25} = Wd$ $\frac{6aW}{4.25} = Wd$ $d = \frac{6a}{4.25}$ $d = \frac{6a}{17/4}$ $d = \frac{24a}{17}$ $d = 1.41a \text{ (3 sf)}$ Or in exact form: $d = \frac{24a}{17}$

Final answer

$d=\dfrac{24a}{17}=1.41a$.

  1. Set the positions relative to C

    The rod is 6.5 m long and overhangs C by 1.5 m, so C is 5.0 m from A. The rod centre of mass is 3.0 m from A, so it is 2.0 m to the left of C. Point D is 0.5 m from A, so it is 4.5 m to the left of C.

  2. Use the limiting balance for smallest M

    For the smallest $M$, the rod is just prevented from tilting clockwise about C. Taking moments about C: $Mg(4.5)+1.2g(2)=4g(1.5)$.

  3. Find M

    $4.5M+2.4=6$, so $4.5M=3.6$ and $M=0.8$.

  4. Place the new particle E

    Now the old particles are removed. Since $EB=0.9$ m and $AB=6.5$ m, E is $5.6$ m from A, so E is $0.6$ m to the right of C.

  5. Find the new particle mass from moments

    At tilting about C, moments about C give $Xg(0.6)=1.2g(2)$, so $X=4$.

  6. Find the force at C

    The upward force at C balances the rod and the new particle: $R_C=1.2g+4g=5.2g\ \mathrm{N}$, which is about $51\ \mathrm{N}$.

Final answer

    Given $\tan\theta=\dfrac34$, use $\sin\theta=\dfrac35$ and $\cos\theta=\dfrac45$.

  2. Resolve horizontally

    The horizontal force of $2$ N is balanced by the horizontal component of the tension in the sloping part of the string: $T\cos\theta=2$.

  3. Find the tension

    $T\cdot\dfrac45=2$, so $T=2.5\ \mathrm{N}$.

  4. Resolve vertically

    The ring is smooth, so the tension is the same in both parts of the string. Upward components are $T$ from the vertical part and $T\sin\theta$ from the sloping part. Therefore $T+T\sin\theta=Mg$.

  5. Find the mass

    $Mg=2.5+2.5\cdot\dfrac35=4.0$, so $M=\dfrac{4.0}{9.8}=0.408\ \mathrm{kg}$.

8. WME01/01 May/June 2023 · Q6

Medium7 marks
Show worked solution
  1. Use the trig ratios

    Given $\tan\alpha=\dfrac43$, use $\sin\alpha=\dfrac45$ and $\cos\alpha=\dfrac35$.

  2. Resolve vertically

    The applied force increases the normal reaction in this model, so $R=W+P\sin\alpha$.

  3. Resolve horizontally

    For the particle not to move, friction must be able to balance the horizontal component of the applied force, so $P\cos\alpha\le F_{\max}$.

  4. Use limiting friction

    Since $\mu=\dfrac14$, $F_{\max}=\dfrac14R=\dfrac14(W+P\sin\alpha)$.

  5. Substitute the exact trig values

    Thus $P\cdot\dfrac35\le\dfrac14\left(W+P\cdot\dfrac45\right)$.

  6. Rearrange the inequality

    Multiplying by $20$ gives $12P\le5W+4P$. Hence $8P\le5W$, so $P\le\dfrac58W$.

Final answer

$P\le\dfrac{5}{8}W$.

    A bearing of $240^\circ$ is $60^\circ$ west of south. Hence $\mathbf{F}=(-8\sin60^\circ)\mathbf{i}+(-8\cos60^\circ)\mathbf{j}=-4\sqrt3\mathbf{i}-4\mathbf{j}$.

    The force $\mathbf{G}$ is due south with magnitude $10$, so $\mathbf{G}=-10\mathbf{j}$. Therefore $\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{F}+\mathbf{G}=-4\sqrt3\mathbf{i}-14\mathbf{j}$.

  3. Find the magnitude

    $|\mathbf{R}|=\sqrt{(-4\sqrt3)^2+(-14)^2}=\sqrt{48+196}=\sqrt{244}\ \mathrm{N}$.

  4. Find the bearing angle

    The resultant points south-west. The angle west of south satisfies $\tan\theta=\dfrac{4\sqrt3}{14}=\dfrac{2\sqrt3}{7}$.

  5. State the bearing

    $\theta=26.3^\circ$. Bearing $=180^\circ+26.3^\circ=206.3^\circ$, so the bearing is $206^\circ$ to the nearest degree.

Final answer

$(i)\ |\mathbf{R}|=\sqrt{244}\ \mathrm{N}\approx15.6\ \mathrm{N}.\quad (ii)\ \text{bearing}=206^\circ$.

    The two given forces have angle $150^\circ$ between them, so the triangle used for adding them has the supplementary angle $30^\circ$ between the side of length $X$ and the side of length $5\sqrt3$.

15. WME01/01 January 2022 · Q5

Medium8 marks · main topic: Resolving Forces, Inclined Planes
Show worked solution
  1. Use the trig ratios

    Since $\tan\alpha=\dfrac34$, $\sin\alpha=\dfrac35$ and $\cos\alpha=\dfrac45$.

  2. Resolve perpendicular to the plane

    The force $X$ acts along the plane, so the normal reaction is $R=mg\cos\alpha=\dfrac45mg$.

  3. Use friction

    The limiting friction is $F=\mu R=\dfrac45\mu mg$.

  4. Use the sliding-up condition

    When $X=2P$, the particle is about to slide up, so friction acts down the plane. Hence $2P=mg\sin\alpha+F=\dfrac35mg+\dfrac45\mu mg$.

  5. Use the sliding-down condition

    When $X=P$, the particle is about to slide down, so friction acts up the plane. Hence $P+F=mg\sin\alpha$, so $P=\dfrac35mg-\dfrac45\mu mg$.

  6. Eliminate P

    Double the second equation: $2P=\dfrac65mg-\dfrac85\mu mg$. This equals the first expression for $2P$.

  7. Solve for mu

    $\dfrac65mg-\dfrac85\mu mg=\dfrac35mg+\dfrac45\mu mg$. Cancel $mg$ and multiply by $5$: $6-8\mu=3+4\mu$. Hence $3=12\mu$ and $\mu=\dfrac14$.

Final answer

$\mu=\dfrac14$.

  1. Set the positions

    Take distances from A. Then C is at $1$ m, D is at $3$ m, the rod's centre of mass is at $2.5$ m, and B is at $5$ m.
